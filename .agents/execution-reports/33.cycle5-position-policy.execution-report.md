## Executive summary

Plan 33 built an offline simulator for the 14-clause position-management policy, drove it from
**real Executor fills** on 84 sessions × 2 DOL tracks, and scored it against the plan's frozen
comparison set. All seven tasks were completed and all six deliverable modules plus their tests
exist and pass.

**The primary finding is about what was measured, not about the capture number.** The policy
did not get to act on most of the corpus, so the study is largely a measurement of the ENTRY
mechanism's stop placement rather than of the management rule it set out to test. Of the 58
stop exits on the production track, **31 fired on the entry mechanism's own initial stop**
before any clause acted; 16 on clause 3, 1 on clause 4, 1 on clause 5, 9 on clause 7. **The
trail — the part under test — set the firing stop on 10 of 66 sessions.** Median time to the
policy's first action is 3.0 min against a median hold of 1.3 min. Plan 33 §9 leads with this
and instructs that the capture figures be read as "what this policy achieved ON TOP OF these
entries", never as "what this policy is worth".

Against that caveat, the plan's own §8 failure condition #1 is met: on the production DOL
track the policy captures **8.80%** of the oracle ceiling (n = 66, 95% session-clustered CI
**[3.03%, 14.91%]**) against **B-tight 7.52%** and **B-loose 11.63%** — beating the weak
baseline by 1.3 points, inside its own interval, and losing to the do-nothing baseline.

Two further results were added after the first draft of this report, at the team lead's
instruction, and are recorded in plan 33 §9:

- **The clause-12 ablation.** Withholding the initial target on every session — deleting
  clauses 6 and 7 — RAISES capture to **0.1007** (production) and **0.1894** (oracle), and
  converts eight oracle stop-outs back into DOL touches. The regime half of the policy is
  net negative on this corpus. Reported as an arm, deliberately kept out of the frozen §6
  comparison set, with a test asserting it cannot leak into it.
- **A cross-study synthesis — "widen and decay; never clamp on an event."** Reconciles this
  ablation with cycle 4's time-conditioned trail, which found tightening BEAT a fixed trail.
  Gradual decay from a wide base helps; sudden event-triggered clamping to a tight level
  hurts. Attributed as a synthesis across two studies, not as a plan-33 result, and carrying
  the cross-basis caveat that the time-conditioning figure is origin-anchored and has never
  been measured on real fills.

**A note on the comparison set.** The three external references in §6 (legacy ladder ~0.44,
best fixed-D 0.4665, best time-conditioned 0.5370) were measured from the SEGMENT ORIGIN — a
lookahead anchor — while 8.80% is from real Executor fills. They are labelled CROSS-BASIS in
§9 and must not be read as "the policy is five times worse than a fixed trail"; an earlier
study measured capture falling 0.457 → 0.094 purely by moving that anchor, with no change to
the exit rule.

## 1. What was built

Six modules and a runner, all under `agent/study/` and `scripts/`, all new, all unstaged.

**`agent/study/entries.py` (571 lines)** — the entry feed. Rebuilds the 1s replay's frame stream
(completed 1m bars plus one running partial minute, once per second) and drives the **real
`agent.trader.executor.Executor`** against a synthetic plan built by the real `derive_plan`,
without running the backtest. Emits the first fill of each session with the mechanism's own stop.
Categorises non-fills as `no_dol`, `dol_behind_at_arm`, `plan_dead`, `no_trigger`. Arms at 09:21 ET.

**`agent/study/legacy_liquidities.py` (161 lines)** — an offline reconstruction of the legacy
`liquidities` list that `compute_cautious_prices` requires. Adopted from another agent's scratch
code and re-validated here: `test_legacy_liquidities.py` pins it against the archived
`<global>/sessions/2026-08-25/daily.json` and asserts the reconstruction reproduces that snapshot
exactly at its own 11:59 boundary — name set, `kind`, and `top`/`bottom` or `price` per liquidity.
A separate test monkeypatches every `smt_state.save_*` to raise, proving the module performs no
indirect legacy write.

**`agent/study/targets_for_policy.py` (221 lines)** — the two DOL tracks and the initial target.
Production DOL is `build_menus` D1 (returned as `dict(menu["rows"][0])`, so it is the menu's own
row and not a second copy of the rule); oracle DOL comes from the label corpus. Initial target is
`compute_cautious_prices`' `cautious_price_initial`, anchored at the fill.

**`agent/study/position_policy.py` (401 lines)** — the simulator for clauses 1-14. 1m logic, 1s
touches, every action effective at the next 1m bar open.

**`agent/study/policy_baselines.py` (260 lines)** — plan 31 §3's B-tight and B-loose, re-run on
the new basis, plus §6's metric machinery (ceiling, capture, scratch, pooled capture, seeded
session-clustered bootstrap, extent terciles). Imports `STOP_CAP_PTS` from the Executor and the
far-edge helper from `position_policy` rather than restating either.

**`scripts/report_position_policy.py` (294 lines)** — the corpus runner. Writes
`.agents/rule-search/position_policy.json`; excludes the acceptance date from every statistic;
freezes the external reference set (0.44 / 0.4665 / 0.5370) as data.

## 4. Divergences from the plan

### Divergence 1 — entry DOL taken at the 09:20 L1 boundary, not at the fill
**Classification:** ✅ GOOD (corrects a defect in the plan).
**Planned:** §2.1 — production DOL = `_dol_menu` D1 "at the fill".
**Actual:** D1 from `build_menus` at the 09:20 L1 boundary (`dol_source == "build_menus_d1_09:20"`);
the fill-time D1 is computed and tested as a separate `d1_at_fill` field but is not what the
corpus carries.
**Reason:** the spec is circular for an entry feed — the fill cannot exist until the Executor has
a DOL, since `dol_floor` veto and `dol_reached` death are computed against it. It is also not what
production does; `target_offline.py` takes the DOL once at 09:20.
**Impact:** neutral for correctness, and it is what makes Task 1 runnable at all. **Cost:** the
corpus run does not carry `d1_at_fill`, so the alternative cannot be recovered from the artifacts
without a re-run.
**Justified:** yes.

### Divergence 2 — arm at 09:21, not 09:20
**Classification:** ✅ GOOD.
**Planned:** `replay.py`'s 09:20 window start.
**Actual:** `ARM_ET == (9, 21)`, tape window 09:21 → 13:00.
**Reason:** production's Executor does not exist at 09:20 — the thesis arrives ~40 s later and the
plan is derived at the next bar close.
**Impact:** small. Verified from the artifacts: **0 of 12** production deaths carry a 09:20-minute
timestamp. The plan's paired claim of "5 of 23 oracle deaths" corresponds exactly to the 5
`dol_behind_at_arm` rows, whose classification is arm-instant-dependent — see §7 below, this is
the one §9 figure not independently checkable from the shipped artifacts.
**Justified:** yes.

### Divergence 3 — oracle DOL restricted to draws still ahead of price at the arm
**Classification:** ✅ GOOD.
**Planned:** oracle DOL from the label corpus, unqualified.
**Actual:** an `is_forward_draw` gate; 5 sessions rejected as `dol_behind_at_arm`.
**Reason:** the label-corpus draw is *the last pool the move reached*, so on part of the corpus it
is already spent at the arm and would be an instant `dol_reached` death rather than a target. The
obvious alternative (use the segment extreme) was considered and rejected because it is touched by
definition, making capture 1.0 by construction and the trail, ratchet and flip untestable.
**Impact:** reduces the oracle track to n = 41 and keeps it a real test.
**Justified:** yes.

### Divergence 4 — DOL menu built at 1m resolution
**Classification:** ⚠️ ENVIRONMENTAL.
**Reason:** at 1s, `facts_source.py`'s 17-day lookback is empty at the start of the study range
(4 levels against 26 on 2026-05-01).
**Impact:** this is the MENU's resolution only. It is distinct from, and does not disturb, §4(c)'s
1m-logic / 1s-touch basis for the policy itself — the artifact records `logic_basis: "1m"`,
`touch_basis: "1s"`.
**Justified:** yes.

### Divergence 5 — plan 31 updated by a new §8, not by an edit to §4
**Classification:** ✅ GOOD.
**Planned:** Task 6 — "note in plan 31 that its §4 stands for 1m-only work."
**Actual:** an append-only §8 (verified: **+57 insertions, 0 deletions**; §4 and §7's precedence
rule byte-identical).
**Impact:** plan 31 remains a stable citation target. Its §8 also discharges §5's 2026-08-03
caveat, which Task 1's real-mechanism fills made possible.
**Justified:** yes.

### Divergence 6 — clause 7 admitted on the flip bar itself
**Classification:** ⚠️ INTERPRETATION (recorded, not silently resolved).
**Reason:** one bar can both close beyond the target and close opposite by ≥3 pts; "post-flip" does
not settle whether clause 7 may act on that same bar.
**Actual:** admitted. Both actions are effective at the same instant under clause 11 and clause 8
takes the tighter, so admitting it can only tighten.
**Impact:** bounded by construction; pinned by a dedicated test. The Sep-2 fixture does not
discriminate the two readings, so this is untested against real evidence.
**Justified:** yes, with the caveat recorded.

## 5. Defects found in post-execution review

Both were found **after** a first set of numbers existed. A full re-run followed; the figures in
§3 are from the corrected run and the first set is superseded. Both fixes are pinned by a named
regression test, and both tests were confirmed present and passing.

### Defect 1 — clause 8's monotone fold was seeded with nothing
`position_policy._monotone` seeded its fold with `None`, so it accepted the first proposal
unconditionally. A regime flip needs no FVG, so clause 7 can *be* that first proposal — and a
wick-anchored ratchet could then move the stop **below** the mechanism's own initial stop,
violating clause 2 and clause 8 together, silently.
**Fix:** seed with the mechanism's stop. **Pinned by:**
`test_clause_7_can_never_loosen_the_mechanisms_own_stop` — a tape where the target clears before
any continuation gap completes and a −6 body wicks to 89 against a 90.0 initial stop; asserts no
clause-3 move precedes it, every move price ≥ 90.0, and the exit is `EXIT_STOP` at exactly 90.0.
**Effect on results:** production capture **0.0780 → 0.0880**.

### Defect 2 — the 1s partial bar carried cumulative extremes for MNQ
`backtest_smt.py:1690` writes the partial minute's MNQ row with **raw per-second** High/Low
(`_fm[_nb_mnq] = [_mo, _mH[_si], _mL[_si], _c, _v]`) while MES keeps cumulative values. `entries.py`
carried cumulative for both. The Executor reads that row for `now_mid` (§11's calibrated
market-fill price) and for the crossed-trigger test, so every market fill was priced on a basis
the mechanisms were never calibrated against.
**Fix:** reproduce the backtest's asymmetry verbatim in `entries._replay_frames` (confirmed
line-for-line against `backtest_smt.py:1686-1691`). **Pinned by:**
`test_the_partial_minute_carries_RAW_per_second_extremes_for_mnq`, which asserts MNQ carries
`[20,12,13]`/`[5,9,8]` and MES the cumulative `[20,20,20]`/`[5,5,5]`.
**Effect on results:** the fill moved on **17 of 168** session-tracks and the outcome category on
2; production yield 77.4% → 78.6%.

Neither reversed the verdict. Both are recorded here because the first run was published before
they were found, not because the delta was interesting.

## 10. What the numbers do and do not support

Carried forward from §9 because it is the part of the result most likely to be misread later:

- **This is not a verdict on the policy's clauses.** Nearly half the corpus never reaches clause 3
  and the trail sets the firing stop on 10 of 66 sessions. What was measured is overwhelmingly the
  **entry mechanism's stop placement**. This corpus cannot separate the two.
- **Clause 1 is load-bearing in a way the plan did not anticipate.** "One position per session,
  the first fill, no re-entry" is what makes a 1.3-minute median hold the whole of a session's
  result. Production would take up to three attempts (`MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3`).
- **Task 1's gate measured the wrong quantity and passed.** Fill yield 78.6% and median room to
  the extreme 204.4 points were both reported before any capture figure, exactly as §8 requires —
  and neither predicted that half those fills would be dead inside a minute. **The gate should

frozen before results existed, and the diagnosis — that the policy is simply slower than the
median trade — is reproducible from the shipped artifacts in a few lines of arithmetic.

**Alignment score: 9/10.** One point off for Divergence 1's cost: the corpus does not carry
`d1_at_fill`, so the plan's literal reading cannot be checked without re-running.

**Ready for production: No — and the plan never proposed it should be.** This is a study. The
policy is not a shippable mechanism on this evidence, and the honest next step is a corpus in
which a position survives long enough for the policy to be the thing under test.
