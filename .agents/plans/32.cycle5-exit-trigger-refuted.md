# Cycle 5 — the pool-sweep exit trigger is REFUTED

**Status: findings. Negative results, recorded because the protocol requires it.**

Companion to `31.cycle5-counterfactual-baseline.md`, which pins every baseline quoted here.
Neither document is complete without the other.

---

## 1. What cycle 5 asked

Position management, assuming direction, entry and target are given: advance the stop to
breakeven, take an early exit at an initial target, ratchet approaching the primary target,
take profit at it.

Reframed early: all four are one question — ***is the move over?*** — asked continuously as
facts arrive, in the same engine as the entry mechanisms rather than at fixed instants.

The user's candidate rule, stated on 2026-08-21 and treated as the pre-registered hypothesis:

> Move the stop to BE once the move has committed. Select a nearer pool as an **initial
> target**. When the 1m bar that sweeps it **closes against the move**, exit at market. When
> it closes *with* the move, hold and re-assess on later bars (**the else-branch**).

Phase-1 discipline was declared before measuring: *if continuation and turn cases overlap, the
honest deliverable is "hold to the stop", and that is a result rather than a failure.*

**That is what happened.**

## 2. The four studies

### 2.1 The initial target's IDENTITY does not matter (n=8 days)

Replacing "only the designated initial target's sweep may fire the trigger" with "any named
pool's sweep may fire it" changed nothing: **same bar, same level, same P&L on 8 of 8 days.**

Structural, not coincidental: the day extreme is a menu row on 6 of 8 days, so the loosened
variant can only fire earlier, and on both firing days the day extreme *was* the first
qualifying pool. The designation had nothing to decide.

**Consequence:** the planned initial-target selection study was cancelled. It would have
optimised a choice with no measurable effect.

Loosening further — dropping the close-against test in favour of "reclaimed the level" — was
**worse**, costing 195 pts on 08-03 alone. The close-direction test was the selective part of
the rule.

### 2.2 The legacy ladder is a real baseline and it is distance-confined (n=8 days)

`hypothesis.compute_cautious_prices` (`hypothesis.py:99`) already derives an initial/secondary
ladder at the fill: candidates capped at `CAUTIOUS_SECONDARY_MAX_DIST = 150` pts, secondary =
farthest inside the cap, initial = farthest nearer than it within `CAUTIOUS_INITIAL_MAX_DIST =
110`, else `synthetic_85pct`.

Reconstructed offline and **validated 20/20 levels against the one archived session snapshot
(2026-08-25), FVG set byte-identical.**

- **Both rungs filled on 8 of 8 days**, all within 25 minutes. Mean **+133.2 pts**, capturing
  **44%** of each day's available move.
- The legacy secondary sits **nearer than the running day extreme on 7 of 8 days** — so the
  ladder structurally cannot express a design whose initial target is the day extreme.
- `synthetic_85pct` fired 1 of 8, via the empty-pool fallback, landing **beyond its own cap**.
- The caps are points and drift **1.83×** across eight days (150 pts = 1.57×–2.86× avg_1h).

**Correction to an earlier claim in this project:** the legacy candidate universe is *richer*
than the DOL menu, not poorer — it carries TDO, TWO, `day_mid`/`week_mid`, and both the high
and the low of every completed session, treating names as opaque (so `asia_high` is a valid
DOWN candidate when it sits below price). It is capped in **distance**, not in membership.
These are different properties and were conflated.

### 2.3 `avg_range_1h` is sound as a normaliser (n=84 sessions)

Recomputed and matched the committed corpus **exactly on 168/168 rows** before use.

- **Predictive, positively**, contrary to the suspicion that it inverts: Spearman **+0.630**
  (MNQ avg_1h vs realised 09:30–16:00 range), +0.444 vs segment excursion; MES +0.582 / +0.451.
  All permutation-significant. Spearman exceeding Pearson throughout indicates heavy tails —
  the quiet-overnight/explosive-open sessions are a real left tail, not the central tendency.
- **As a normaliser** (dispersion of anchor→draw distance, QCD, lower better): pooled
  **0.789 → 0.396**. Unambiguous, and needs no statistics — MNQ's median draw distance is
  212.25 pts against MES's 23.50.
- **Within-instrument the case is unproven**: point estimates favour it on both instruments,
  but every bootstrap CI over sessions straddles zero at n=64/59.
- `avg_range_4h` is a live competitor — better on MNQ (0.294 vs 0.364), worse on MES, a tie
  pooled. **Not** recommended for change: switching denominators would invalidate every
  constant already calibrated in avg_1h units. `prev RTH range` is worse than raw points on MNQ.

**Verdict: ratios stand.** Re-expressing points constants in avg_1h units is justified — on the
cross-instrument evidence, not the within-instrument evidence.

### 2.4 The else-branch has NO trigger (n=84 sessions) — the decisive result

Menu built at a fixed **09:32** clock anchor (plan 30's precedent), sweeps walked forward,
outcomes measured as remaining excursion to the primary segment's extreme in avg_1h units.

**Coverage — the dominant fact:**

| stage | n |
|---|---|
| primary segments, status ok | 84 |
| degenerate at 09:32 (no eligible pool) | 7 |
| menu present but **zero sweeps during the move** | **37** |
| **sessions with no sweep event at all** | **44 (52%)** |
| sweep events (first-touch, projections excluded) | 78 |
| closed **with** the move → else-branch | 67 (86%) |
| closed **against** → the specified trigger | 11 (14%) |

The zero-sweep sessions are **not** quiet: median move extent **2.24 × avg_1h** against 3.51×
for sessions that did sweep, at the same median menu size. The moves happened; the pools were
out of reach.

**Discrimination — none:**

| split | median remaining excursion | 95% CI on the difference |
|---|---|---|
| closed with vs against | 0.68× vs 0.54× | **[−0.92, +2.74]** |
| MES declined vs confirmed | 1.00× vs 0.59× | **[−0.33, +1.34]** |

Band, tier, elapsed time, bar range, distance ratio and prior-sweep count all produce
session-clustered CIs spanning zero. The close-direction result also **flips sign** depending
on whether the outcome is anchored at the bar's close or its extreme, which alone disqualifies
it.

**And the sweep is not where moves end.** Events known *by hindsight* to be the last sweep
before the turn still show only a **55%** turn rate within 0.5× avg_1h — the final sweep was
typically followed by another 0.49× of move.

## 3. The Aug-21 case falsifies its own narrative

The rule and this cycle's MES hypothesis both originated on 2026-08-21, where MNQ swept its day
low at 09:54 and MES sat 15.25 pts (1.51 × its own avg_1h) above its day low, never testing it.
That was read as a contemporaneous bullish SMT naming the turn.

The corpus shows **both** of that session's sweeps carry the same feature:

```
09:37  london(cur)_low   closed_with=True    mes=declined (1.71x)   remaining 1.44x
09:54  asia(cur)_low      closed_with=False   mes=declined (1.51x)   remaining 0.54x
```

The divergence was equally present seventeen minutes earlier, when the move had another
1.44 × avg_1h left. **The feature did not select a bar; it selected both.** At corpus scale its
point estimate runs *opposite* to the hypothesis.

Recorded prominently because the observation was given real weight in this cycle's reasoning
before it was tested, and because the failure mode — a feature that looks decisive on the date
that generated it — is one this project has paid for repeatedly.

## 4. What this kills and what survives

**Killed:**

- the pool-sweep exit trigger, as a primary mechanism, on two independent grounds: **silent on
  52% of sessions**, and **non-discriminating when it fires**;
- the initial-target selection study (§2.1);
- the MES-non-confirmation exit feature, in the form tested.

**Survives:**

- **stop management**, which exists on 100% of sessions;
- `avg_range_1h` as the denominator;
- the legacy ladder as a measured baseline (44% capture, fills 8/8);
- the pinned baselines in plan 31.

**One qualification, which is not a rescue.** These studies measured whether the features
predict *the extreme*. A rule's actual job is to **beat the alternative**, and on 08-21 the
user's rule beat pinned baseline B-tight by 45 pts while still leaving 0.54× on the table.
Those are different questions and the second is unmeasured at corpus scale. It does not save
the trigger as a primary mechanism — the coverage gap is fatal on its own — but whether it
adds value on the minority of sessions where it fires remains open.

## 5. The leading hypothesis going forward

> **Position management is stop management. There is no evidence-driven exit trigger.**

Grounds: it is what the evidence supports; a stop exists on every session while sweep events
exist on 48%; it covers three of the four operations cycle 5 was scoped around (BE, the
tightening, the approach ratchet — only the market-exit was a trigger); and there is measurable
room in it — pinned baseline **B-tight stops on 8 of 8 days, every one within 26 minutes of the
fill**, capturing +132 on 08-21 against 249 available.

The deliverable becomes a **ratchet schedule** — how tight, anchored on what, advancing on what
— scored as capture against scratch rate, bracketed by B-tight and B-loose.

**Secondary observation, deliberately OUT of cycle 5's scope:** coverage is the binding
constraint. 52% of sessions produce no sweep; 08-12's menu offered nothing reachable; 08-24 had
an 803-pt hole between D1 and D2 and turned inside it. The same finding has now arrived from
three independent directions. It is a facts/target problem, not a management one, and belongs
to whichever cycle owns the candidate universe.

## 6. What would overturn this

- **A different move-end definition.** "Turn" here is the primary segment's extreme from a
  skeleton built on a 09:30–13:00 window, and sweeps are measured to 13:00 for consistency with
  it. Another definition could change every number in §2.4.
- **A different anchor.** The menu is fixed at 09:32; 33 of 84 primaries begin later, so for
  those the menu predates the move. The anchor-in-move subset (24 sessions, 44 events) shows
  the same nulls but is small.
- **Effective n is sessions, not events.** 67 closed-with events live in 38 sessions; the 11
  closed-against in 9. These CIs rule out a *large* effect, not a small one — particularly for
  the MES comparison.
- **Event families not keyed on pool sweeps** — FVG-based, structural break, displacement decay,
  time-based — are untested. The null covers pool sweeps only.
- MNQ only, 1m resolution, no costs or slippage, oracle-optimal fills on the eight-day studies.
