# Cycle 5 — the counterfactual baseline, pinned

**Status: definition, fixed before further measurement. Nothing here is a proposal.**

Every claim in cycle 5 takes the form *"exiting here beat what the position would otherwise
have done."* That second half is the baseline. It has to be **one** definition, fixed in
advance, or results measured on different days do not compose.

---

## 1. Why this document exists

Two independent reads of 2026-08-21 scored the same counterfactual — hold with a structural
stop, short from 29469.25 — at **+139.00** and at **+70.00**.

Neither was a mistake by the reader. Both followed the instruction "the stop sits just beyond
the last 1m continuation FVG **before the exit**", and they were evaluating *different* exits:

| read | exit being evaluated | last FVG before it | stop | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| trigger-dependency | the user's rule, 09:54 | completed 09:52 | 29330.25 | +139.00 |
| legacy-ladder | legacy secondary, 09:37 | completed 09:33 | 29399.25 | +70.00 |

**The defect is in the instruction, not the execution.** Anchoring the baseline to "the exit"
makes it a function of the candidate being tested, so every rule gets its own baseline and the
comparisons are circular.

**And both were wrong anyway.** A stop that only ever tightens — which is what a trailing stop
is — reaches 29337.25 at 09:44 and is touched at **09:45**, before either quoted exit existed:

```
ratchet: 09:32 -> 29416.25   09:33 -> 29399.25   09:44 -> 29337.25
touched: 09:45 (high 29353.75)      P&L = +132.00
```

Freezing the trail at whichever FVG preceded the candidate exit is what produced both numbers.

---

## 2. The rule this pins

> **A baseline never references the candidate exit's time, price, or existence.** It is a
> self-contained policy that runs from the fill to its own termination, and it produces the
> same number no matter which rule is being compared against it.

---

## 3. The two baselines

Both run on **MNQ 1m bars**, from the fill to **16:00 ET**. Report every result against **both**;
they bracket the answer and neither alone is honest.

### 3.1 Shared conventions

- **Fill price** = the *open* of the 1m bar containing the entry instant. Uniform across days.
- **Initial stop** = fill ± `STOP_CAP_PTS`, adverse side. **`STOP_CAP_PTS = 25.0`**, taken from
  production (`agent/trader/executor.py:66`) rather than invented here. It is a *points*
  constant and inherits the points-vs-ratio defect recorded in §6.
- **Stops are TOUCH-based**, not close-based: `high >= stop` for a short, `low <= stop` for a
  long. This matches `agent/trader/order_sim.py`, which is what a resting stop order does. See
  §6 for the doctrinal tension this creates.
- **Same-bar ambiguity resolves adversely**, as `order_sim.py` already requires.
- A position that never stops is **marked to the 16:00 close and labelled `MARK`**. A mark is
  **not an exit** and may never be quoted as one — this is the artifact that made an earlier
  read appear to show the user's trigger losing on 08-24.

### 3.2 The FVG definition — taken from the codebase, not restated

From `agent/facts/detectors/fvg.py`, mirroring `derive_facts.fvgs`:

- **bear gap**: `bar3.High < bar1.Low`, spanning `[bar3.High, bar1.Low]`
- **bull gap**: `bar3.Low > bar1.High`, spanning `[bar1.High, bar3.Low]`
- the three bars must be adjacent on the tf grid **and inside one CME session**
  (`_window_is_contiguous`) — a window straddling the 16:55–18:00 break is a hole in the tape,
  not an imbalance
- the gap **completes at bar 3**, and is usable only from that bar onward

The **far edge** (the adverse side, where the stop goes) is `bar1.Low` for a bear gap and
`bar1.High` for a bull gap.

### 3.3 B-tight — the ratcheting FVG trail

The protective policy.

1. Stop starts at the initial stop (§3.1).
2. When a same-direction 1m FVG **completes**, move the stop to its far edge **if and only if
   that is tighter** than the current stop. Monotone: the stop never loosens.
3. Exit when touched; otherwise `MARK`.

### 3.4 B-loose — the unmanaged hold

The initial stop, never moved. Exit when touched; otherwise `MARK`. This is a **ceiling**, not
a policy: it terminates on its own on 1 of 8 days.

## 5. What these numbers say about the baselines themselves

**B-tight stops on 8 of 8 days, every one of them within 26 minutes of the fill.** On 08-21 it
exits at 09:45 while the move runs on to 10:21. The FVG trail is **scratch-prone**: it is
exactly the "false exit on a winner" failure cycle 5 exists to avoid, and any evidence-driven
exit rule will beat it easily.

**So B-tight is a weak baseline and beating it is cheap.** That is why B-loose is reported
alongside it: B-loose is generous (it never stops, so it banks the best of the rest of the
session), and a rule worth shipping should sit between them rather than merely above B-tight.

**08-03 is a convention artifact, not a result.** The 25-pt initial stop is touched inside the
entry bar itself. An oracle "best fill before the extreme" placed at the 1m *open* is not a
fill any mechanism would have taken; production enters on a stop-entry at a trigger. Treat
08-03 as unusable for baseline comparison until fills come from real mechanism triggers.

## 6. Known weaknesses, recorded rather than fixed

- **`STOP_CAP_PTS = 25.0` is a points constant.** On this corpus MNQ's `avg_range_1h` ranged
  52.40–95.76, so 25 pts is 0.26×–0.48× — nearly a factor of two. Same defect as the legacy
  150/110/40 caps. Left as-is deliberately: it is production's number, and the baseline's job
  is to reproduce production, not to improve it.
- **Touch-based stops contradict the FVG close-through doctrine.** `fvg.py` holds that a gap
  dies only when a bar *closes* beyond its far end — "a gap that was wicked through but repelled
  the close is a *proven* barrier, not a dead one". A touch-based stop dies on the wick. Both
  are defensible; touch is chosen because it models a resting stop order, which is what actually
  exists at a broker. A close-based variant is a legitimate future bracket.
- **No costs, no slippage, one instrument, 1m resolution.** Fills at exact prices.
- **n = 8 hand-picked days.** These are calibration figures, not evidence.

### B-tight is unchanged, to the cent, on all eight days

Every value in §4's B-tight column reproduces exactly; the only thing 1s adds is the
SECOND of each exit. That is not luck, and the reason matters more than the result: **a 1m
bar's High is the maximum of its constituent 1s Highs and its Low the minimum**, so a touch
the 1m frame finds is a touch the 1s frame finds, and vice versa. Bar resolution cannot
change *whether* a stop fires — only *when*, within the minute.

**So §4 (c) of plan 33 is right in principle and its correction is empirically zero.** The
concern that "the comparison drifts" if the baselines stay on 1m does not materialise on
this data, and the re-run's value is that it establishes that rather than assuming it.

### The B-loose differences are a MARK convention, not a resolution effect

Seven of eight B-loose figures differ from §4 by −34.00 to +16.00 (−2.1% in total), and
none of it comes from the re-basis: **the 1m-touch and 1s-touch runs return identical
numbers** (both total +1280.50). Every difference is in the price the un-stopped position is
MARKED at — which "the 16:00 close" of §3.1 does not disambiguate between the last print at
or before 16:00 and the close of the bar labelled 16:00. `policy_baselines` uses the former.

The stop/`MARK` classification reproduces §4 exactly on all eight days, 08-03's stop
included, so nothing here disturbs §5's readings — including that B-tight stops 8 of 8
within 26 minutes, and that 08-03 is a convention artifact.

**08-03's caveat is now discharged.** §5 says to treat it as unusable "until fills come from
real mechanism triggers". Plan 33 Task 1 supplies exactly that, and plan 33's corpus
comparison runs both baselines from the real Executor fills rather than from an oracle
1m-open fill.
