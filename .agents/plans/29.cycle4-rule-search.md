## Global Constraints

- **Result fields are forbidden to every predictor.** `overshoot`, `lag_min`, `own_extreme`
  and `extreme_ts` are computed from the move's end. A rule that reads one is not a rule
  (§10). Only `start_ts`, the entry-proxy instants, and facts evaluated at or before them.
- **The labelling is not revised by this phase.** If a rule disagrees with a label, that is
  evidence about the rule.
- **The holdout is touched once**, in Stage C, after the survivor list is final. 21 sessions
  is the entire budget and each peek spends it.
- Rules are judged on naming the draw, never on P&L (§10).
- No Executor code. Phases 1–4 produce documents and data.
- Bar time only; the gate over `agent/study` already enforces it.

## Why Stage A comes before any search

Phase 2 left two headline results that are confounds, and one structural fact that reframes
the whole problem.

**The target is ordinal, not categorical.** The label is the *last* eligible candidate the
move reached. Predicting it is therefore predicting **how far the move will go, counted in
pools cleared** — not which pool is intrinsically special. The cheapest theory that could be
true is "the move clears k pools", and no other theory means anything until that one has been
measured.

**Class pull is confounded by proximity (G1).** The classes that appeared to draw hardest are
exactly the classes that sit nearest: mean normalised distance rank open_price 0.13,
session_extreme 0.21, day_extreme 0.52, week_extreme 0.81 — the identical ordering to the
apparent pull. Spec §3.4 is untested, not supported.

**Null A is partly mechanical (G2).** "Last reached" is by construction the furthest-along
pool the move crossed, so it must beat a null drawn from the whole eligible set, which
includes pools the move never approached.

This project's expensive failures share one shape: a result that scored well and was an
artefact — GIL-24 (raw SMT 54% until the daily-trend split), GIL-17/18/19 (relevance gates
that were regime effects), the ATH-rejection 1m +$3,862 that evaporated at 1s. G1 and G2 are
that shape and are already visible. Building on them wastes the corpus.

### A3 — G2 is worse than diagnosed: Null A should be retired

The reached-only null still returns `beats_null_frac = 1.0` (MNQ observed 53.25 vs null 124.0,

measures the monotonicity of moves and nothing about the label's quality.

Null A is therefore withdrawn as evidence for the labelling, and Stage B scores against the

correction to this plan's own Stage A design, made before any rule was searched for.

### What Stage A changes about Stage B

The ordinal framing has turned the cycle's question into a sharper one. reach-m says the draw
is the furthest pool within `m x avg_range_1h`, with a single per-session parameter — so a
Stage B family is now **a rule that predicts today's m** from prior-state facts. That is the
"reasonable distance" question in its operational form, and it is exactly the shape of the
hypothesis behind this cycle: some pools draw harder, and how far the move runs depends on
where the hard-drawing pool sits.

## Stage A addendum (2026-09-04) — B4 is UN-struck; the effect is geometric, not categorical

Striking B4 on A2's evidence was an over-reach and is reversed here.

A2 tested the **marginal** class effect, `P(draw | class)` with distance held fixed. That is
what phase 2 claimed and it does not survive. It is not what spec §3.4 needs to mean. The
hypothesis that matters is **conditional**: a pool's weight depends on the stack around it and
on cross-instrument state at the moment it is tested. A marginal test averages those away and
could not have detected them either way.

### The chaining effect survives the confound that killed the marginal one

For every eligible pool, "did the move cross it", with **distance from origin held fixed** and
the gap to the pool behind it varied:

| | dist 2-3x, gap <= 0.5x | dist 2-3x, gap > 0.5x |
|---|---|---|
| MNQ | **94.1%** (n=17) | 55.6% (n=18) |
| MES | **50.0%** (n=20) | 26.3% (n=19) |

Same direction on both instruments, in the 2-3x band where the reach-m optimum lives. A pool
sitting close behind another is crossed far more often than one standing alone at the same
distance. Beyond 3x everything collapses (MNQ 10.2% vs 7.5%, MES 5.7% vs 8.4% — both tiny,
and MES reverses), so the effect is confined to the reachable band.

**Tier, by contrast, still shows nothing once distance is controlled**: MNQ at 2-3x is session
77.8% (n=18) against day 75.0% (n=12); MES is session 25.0% (n=12) against day 30.0% (n=20).
So "pools are not equal" is real, but it is showing up as **geometry — clustering and chaining
— not as class identity**.

### The specific named configuration is real and rare

Week-tier pools sit a median **11.7 x avg_1h** from the origin on MNQ (MES 10.0); only 9% are
within 3x at all. The configuration "a day/session pool within 3x with a week pool <= 0.5x
beyond it" occurs on **4 of 48** MNQ segments — and the move continued to the week pool on
**all 4** (MES: 7 of 44, continued on 5).

4/4 with a mechanism is a **named case**, not a rate. It goes in the phase-4 registry, and n=4
is stated beside it every time it is quoted.

### A null I ran and am discarding

The first chaining test compared the stopping gap against a length-biased ("inspection
paradox") null, which came out 2.30x against 4.68x — apparently refuting chaining. That null
is contaminated: it averages over gaps of 10-30 x avg_1h out in the unreachable tail, which the
move could never have stopped in. It is reported here only so the discard is on the record; the
distance-controlled table above is the sound version.

### Stage B, restated as a grammar

A rule is `(configuration predicate at instant T) -> (modifier to the baseline ranking)`, with
two kinds of T, which the worked examples separate cleanly:

- **selection**, at the decision instant — about the geometry of the stack;
- **switch**, at a named structural event such as the sweep of the near pool — about
  cross-instrument state that does not exist yet at the decision instant.

| # | Family | Kind | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B8** | chain/cluster: extend the reach-m pick while the next gap <= g | selection | **supported by the table above**; one parameter; the leading candidate |
| **B9** | counterpart-divergence veto: leading asset wicks the day mid and closes back inside while the lagger sweeps -> do NOT extend | switch | needs three new detectors: leader/lagger identity, wick-and-close-back at a mid, SMT at the pool |
| B4 | class / tier | selection | **un-struck, demoted**: no marginal effect survives; admitted only in interaction with B8 |
| B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B7 | as before | — | unchanged |

**The n ceiling, stated before searching.** 48 MNQ discovery segments. B8's effect rests on 35

## Stage A addendum 2 (2026-09-04) — the cross-instrument halt test

Hypothesis under test: *when one asset halts between two of its own pools, that is usually
because the other asset reached one of ITS pools.* 58-60 discovery segments, both instruments,
holdout untouched.

### Method, and a null that had to be fixed first

Distance from the halt to the instrument's nearest own pool, in that instrument's own
`avg_range_1h`. The first null drew a random price from **the MNQ-derived move extent applied
to MES prices** — a range roughly 4x too wide for MES — and produced a nonsense MES null of
8.71. Corrected by reconstructing each instrument's own origin from its own candidate
`dist_from_start`, and separately by measuring MES at **MNQ's exact halt minute** from the 1m
bars rather than by comparing extremes over a shared window.

### Result 1 — halts sit closer to pools than chance, and MES far more so

| | actual | null (random price / minute in the same move) |
|---|---|---|
| MNQ halt → nearest own pool | 0.63 | 0.71 |
| MES halt → nearest own pool | 0.40 | 0.70 |
| MES **at MNQ's halt minute** → nearest MES pool | **0.46** | **0.66** |

So the instant MNQ stops is a moment when MES is unusually close to its own structure. The
pair halts at structure together.

### Result 2 — the COMPENSATING form of the claim is not supported

| condition | other asset at a pool | base | lift |
|---|---|---|---|
| MNQ halt > 0.50 from its own pools (n=31) | MES <= 0.25 on 42% | 40% | **+2 pp** |
| MNQ halt > 0.50 (n=31) | MES <= 0.50 on 52% | 52% | **-0 pp** |
| MNQ halt > 0.75 (n=24) | MES <= 0.25 on 46% | 40% | +6 pp |
| MNQ halt > 1.00 (n=19) | MES <= 0.25 on 53% | 40% | +13 pp |

Flat at moderate thresholds; the lift only appears in the thinnest cell. If "MES explains the
halt when MNQ has no pool" were the dominant mechanism, the lift would be large everywhere.
It is not. The two assets' halt-at-pool events are close to independent rather than
compensating. Recorded as a **negative result**, with the monotone trend (+2 → +6 → +13) noted
as unresolved at n=19.

### Result 3 — the finding that DID come out of it: MES is the better-anchored instrument

MES halts a median 0.40-0.46 avg_1h from its own pools against MNQ's 0.63, and MES's separation
from its null is far wider (0.46 vs 0.66) than MNQ's (0.63 vs 0.71).

The obvious objection is scaling, and it runs the wrong way: MNQ/MES price ratio is ~3.83 while
`avg_range_1h` is 67.8 vs 9.8, a ratio of 6.9 — so MES's hourly range is proportionally
*smaller*, which INFLATES MES distances in avg_1h units. MES looks better despite a handicap,
so the asymmetry is real and if anything understated.

**Consequence for Stage B.** Family B3 (counterpart geometry) is promoted and its hypothesis is
rewritten: not "consult MES when MNQ has no nearby pool" (Result 2 refutes that) but **"MES's
pool structure is the better reference for the pair's target, on every session"** — with the
MNQ target derived from it by price translation. That is a rule with n=44-48 behind it rather
than a residual case with n=19, and it is testable directly against the reach-m baseline.

## B.0 The reformulation: a per-pool hazard, not a per-segment target

Adopted 2026-09-04. The two phrasings — "which pool is the target" and "which pools will it
sweep through before the one it halts at" — describe the same outcome for a monotone move, but
they are different estimation problems and the second is better on three counts:

1. **Units.** 48 MNQ segments become 106 pool-observations. Only 2.2x, because the draw sits at
   median rank 1, but real.
2. **Locality.** "Will it continue past THIS pool?" is answered by facts about that pool — its
   distance, the gaps either side, its tier, what the counterpart has at the same place. A
   global ranking over a variable-length list is a harder object to featurise and to falsify.
3. **It unifies §7's architecture.** A per-pool continue/stop estimate is exactly what a
   switching rule consumes, so the one-off at the fill and the mid-move switch become the SAME
   estimator at different instants with different information, not two mechanisms.

**Target:** `h(pool) = P(the move stops at this pool | it reached this pool)`.

**Everything already built survives the change.** reach-m is a degenerate hazard (0 below the
multiple, 1 above it); chaining is a hazard that depends on the gap ahead.

## B.1 Scoring, fixed before any family is fitted

- **Primary metric: induced top-1**, so it stays comparable with Stage A. Compose the hazard
  along the stack into a stopping distribution, take the argmax, compare to the phase-2 label.
  **The bar is Stage A's: MNQ 56.2%, MES 54.5%.**
- **Secondary: per-pool Brier score**, which sees calibration the argmax hides.
- **Leave-one-session-out.** The hazard is estimated without the session it is scored on. With
  45 usable segments, in-sample scoring would be meaningless.
- **Bootstrap over SESSIONS, never over pool-observations.** 106 observations come from 48
  sessions and are not independent — all pools before the stop are "continue" by construction.
  An observation-level interval is far too narrow.
- **Coverage is reported with every accuracy.** A family that scores better by predicting on
  fewer sessions has not improved.
- Discovery only. The 21 holdout sessions are spent once, in Stage C.

## B.4 Two deliverables, not one

**The n ceiling, stated before searching.** 45-48 usable discovery segments. B8 rests on ~97
pool-observations and can carry a rate. A configuration firing on 4-12 segments cannot, at any
confidence.

So Stage B produces:
1. **Rules with rates** — the families general enough to score against the baseline under LOSO.
2. **A named-case registry** — configurations too rare to rate, each with its mechanism, its
   sessions listed, and its n printed beside it every time it is quoted. The 4/4 day-then-week
   chain and B9's divergence veto are the first two entries.

A registry entry is not a weaker rule. It is the honest form of a real pattern with n=4.

## B.5 RESULT — B8 confirmed, and it is simpler than expected (2026-09-04)

Discovery only, holdout untouched. LOSO induced top-1; intervals bootstrap over sessions.

### The permutation test B8 was gated on

Rank-stratified, 2000 shuffles of the stop/continue labels within each rank:

| | stoppers' median gap ahead | continuers' | |
|---|---|---|---|
| MNQ rank 0 / 1 / 2 | 1.72 / 1.45 / 1.77 | 0.69 / 0.30 / 0.37 | |
| MES rank 0 / 1 | 1.96 / 1.40 | 0.60 / 0.38 | |

Observed stopper-minus-continuer median gap **+1.34 (MNQ) / +1.30 (MES)** against null medians
+0.13 / +0.10, **p < 0.0005 on both**. Consistent at every rank. The "wide gaps catch stops by
construction" explanation is excluded.

### Scores

| family | MNQ acc | MNQ Brier | MES acc | MES Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 — distance only | 41.7% | 0.2498 | 47.7% | 0.2631 |
| B8 — distance + gap ahead | 62.5% | 0.1856 | 65.9% | 0.1852 |
| **B8g — gap ahead ALONE** | **62.5%** | **0.1746** | **68.2%** | **0.1770** |
| B8xB4 — + tier | 58.3% | 0.1835 | 65.9% | 0.2117 |

- **B8 - B0: +20.8 pp [+10.4, +31.2] (MNQ), +18.2 pp [+9.1, +27.3] (MES)**, P(>0) ~ 1.0.
- **vs the Stage A bar (56.2 / 54.5): B8g is +6.3 pp and +13.7 pp.** And the comparison

- **B4 dies a second death.** As an interaction with the gap it costs 4.2 pp on MNQ

### The rule, as a sentence

**The move keeps going while the next pool is close, and stops when the next one is more than
about 1.5 hourly ranges away — regardless of how far it has already travelled.**

Fitted hazards (MNQ / MES): gap <= 0.5x → 0.10-0.42; 0.5-1.5x → 0.15-0.62; > 1.5x → 0.48-0.85.

**Distance from the origin earns no place in the rule.** B8g was declared as a simplification
test after the fitted B8 table showed the hazard varying strongly down the gap column and
weakly across the distance rows — model selection between two encodings of one confirmed
hypothesis, not a new family. Gap-only matches B8 on MNQ with a better Brier and beats it on
MES on both.

That is a direct reversal of Stage A's own framing: reach-m is a *distance* rule and it is
beaten by a rule that ignores distance entirely. "How far will it go" was the wrong question;
"where does the structure thin out" is the right one.

### A convergence worth noting

MES scores 68.2% against MNQ's 62.5%, and addendum 2 independently found MES halts far closer
to its own pools than MNQ does (0.46 vs 0.63 against nulls of 0.66 vs 0.71). Two unrelated
measurements agree that **MES carries the structure**. B3 — MES as the reference instrument —
is next, and now has two supports rather than one.

## B.6 RESULT — B3 REFUTED, and why its two supports were misleading (2026-09-04)

B3's hypothesis after addendum 2: *MES's pool structure anchors the pair's target, so MNQ's
stopping should be predicted by MES's geometry at the translated price.* Implemented as
`alt_gap_ahead` — each pool's price translated into the other instrument's space by the
session's own origin ratio, then the gap to the next pool beyond it there.

| family | MNQ acc | Brier | MES acc | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B8g (own gap only) | **62.5%** | 0.1746 | **68.2%** | 0.1770 |
| B3 (counterpart gap only) | 39.6% | 0.2559 | 47.7% | 0.2462 |
| B8g+B3 | 50.0% | 0.1912 | 63.6% | 0.1947 |

**B8g+B3 - B8g: -12.5 pp [-20.8, -6.2] (MNQ), -4.5 pp [-11.4, +0.0] (MES).** Adding the
counterpart's geometry makes the rule WORSE on both instruments. B3 alone is 20-23 pp behind.

The raw separation was there and was weak — stopper-minus-continuer median counterpart gap
+0.53 (MNQ) and +0.16 (MES) against +1.35 for the own gap — and it was nearly orthogonal to
the own gap (r = 0.16 / 0.01), which is why it looked worth testing. It does not survive.

**Why the two supports were misleading, stated plainly.** B3 was promoted on two findings:
MES scores higher than MNQ under B8g, and MES halts closer to its own pools than MNQ does.
Both are statements about **MES explaining MES**. Neither implies MES explains MNQ, and I
read them as if they did. The direct test was always the only test.

**One caveat, which does not rescue it.** B8g+B3 has 12 buckets against B8g's 4, and at ~45
sessions under LOSO the extra fragmentation costs precision. So the counterpart may carry
signal this encoding cannot extract at this n. Under the pre-registered scoring it fails, and
the pre-registered scoring is the test.

## B.7 The unexplained sessions are NOT explained by the counterpart

Asked directly: are the sessions where one instrument reached no pool explained by the other
reaching one? Contingency over 63 discovery segments:

| | MES labelled | MES unexplained |
|---|---|---|
| MNQ labelled | 37 | 11 |
| MNQ unexplained | 7 | 8 |

- P(MES labelled) = 70% overall, **47% given MNQ unexplained — lift -23 pp** (n=15).
- P(MNQ labelled) = 76% overall, **58% given MES unexplained — lift -18 pp** (n=19).
- Both unexplained on 8 segments where independence predicts 4.5.

The two instruments' failures are **positively correlated, not compensating**. "Unexplained"
is a property of the SESSION, not of the instrument.

That is a more useful finding than a confirmation would have been, because it redirects the
search. The remaining candidates for those sessions are the classes this study deliberately
excluded — **FVG zones** (86 per boundary, held out of the universe so they could not swamp
the null) and **B7's stretch mid** — or a non-price cause such as a time boundary. Testing the
excluded classes **on the unexplained sessions only** bounds the multiple-comparison cost to a
subset that is currently at zero explanatory power, which is the cheapest possible place to
admit them.
