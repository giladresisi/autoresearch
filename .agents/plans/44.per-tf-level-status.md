## 1. The rule to build — V1d, pinned (the REFERENCE DEFINITION)

Reference: `variants.py::_v1(kw, rec, dedup=True)` (= `v1d_per_tf_dedup`), plus the
tie-break in step 4 below. Pure over the 09:20 score kwargs. No bars, no clock.

Per asset `a` in (MNQ, MES):

1. `U[a]` = `suppressed_p1_levels[a]` ∪ `p1_stale_levels[a]` ∪ `suppressed_p2_sites[a]`.
2. Remove every **live P2 site**: `(a, n)` where some `smt_candidates` entry has
   `swept_ticker == a`, `level == n`, `meaningful == True`, and `n` is not in
   `suppressed_p2_sites[a]`. (Such a level already scores its P2 read, and V1d leaves it
   exactly as V0 does. Blanking its 1h would delete a live P2.)
3. For side in (`high`, `low`), the candidates are `n ∈ U[a]` with `n.endswith("_" + side)`,
   `level_htf_close_status[a][n]["4h"] is not None`, and `level_tiers[a][n]["price"] is not None`.
   The side is read from the level NAME, not from the level's `side` field. Names without a
   `_high`/`_low` suffix, and the mids, are never candidates.
4. **Keep exactly one per (a, side): the most extreme price** (max for high, min for low).
   **Tie-break (PINNED here; the study left it to set-iteration order):** higher tier first
   (`level_tiers[a][n]["tier"]`, post-promotion; week 3 > day 2 > session 1), then the first
   name in `level_tiers[a]` insertion order. That order equals `bundle.levels` order, the same
   order `_extremity_shadowed_levels` falls back on.
5. For the kept level: remove it from all three sets for `a` (P1 allowed, P2 allowed,
   staleness ignored), and **retire its 1h** (score nothing on 1h). Its 4h scores through the
   ordinary machinery, unchanged: the P1/P2 dispatch on the effective verdict, the §10
   partial-bar ladder (discount x0.5 / omit / **reverse**), magnitude, the tier ladder with
   §2.1e week-confluence, tf-dedup, dominance, and the §6 contradiction tally.
6. Every other level keeps its V0 treatment on every timeframe.

**Why the pin matters.** The tie at the extreme occurs on **104 / 685** dates (e.g.
`prev2_week_high == prev6_day_high`). `max()` over a set of strings depends on
`PYTHONHASHSEED`. Re-running the unpinned V1d reproduces `per_date.jsonl` nets on only 657 /
637 dates (seeds 0 / 1). With the pin above, the result is hash-seed stable. It matches the
recorded V1d **call on all 685 dates**, and the **net on 682**. The 3 net differences are
all tie-order cases with an unchanged call:
`2024-07-29` −12.5625 -> −14.25 DOWN; `2025-03-17` −0.375 -> −0.5625 NEUTRAL;
`2026-06-03` −6.0 -> −6.5625 DOWN. Every headline figure below is identical under the pin.

## 2. Rule-level ambiguities (V1d answer · is it evidence or convenience · pin)

**(a) What retires a level on a timeframe.**
- V1d: for the restored level, only the 4h read stands. The 1h is retired unconditionally.
  Un-restored suppressed levels stay retired on both tfs. Un-suppressed levels are untouched
  on both.
- Reconciling the operator's phrasing: "ignored on TFs where a bar closed beyond them"
  cannot be read literally. A close beyond IS the accept/reject evidence. Read literally, it
  would retire the 4h on 09-28 (two 4h closes below) and keep the 1h reclaim, which is the
  opposite of the operator's own "4h standing acceptance" reading. The brief's own table
  treats the 1h as "resolved as a reclaim", i.e. not scored. V1d matches that. When the 1h
  AGREES with the 4h, retiring it changes nothing, because tf-dedup already keeps only the
  4h. So V1d's retirement bites exactly when the 1h has closed back (the operator's
  "resolved" case).
- Evidence: keeping the 1h (alternative a) is worse on every cut, −0.9 pp.
- Pin: the V1d answer. The rule is "the stack keeps one standing 4h read", not a general
  per-tf retirement engine. A level with a mature 1h but an immature 4h is NOT restored. That
  case is unmeasured and would be a new variant.

**(b) De-dup.**
- V1d: one restoration per (asset, side), the most extreme price among suppressed levels
  with a mature 4h. It is not compared against un-suppressed live levels, so a restored level
  can sit shallower than a live same-side level.
- The pick can be a level that §2.1b calls nested. On 09-28 `prev3_day_low` wins while
  nested under the unswept `prev2_day_low`. That is the point: the deepest crossed level
  carries the stack's vote.
- Evidence: "only if beyond every live level" (b) is slightly worse. The tie-break was a
  convenience and is now pinned (§1).
- Pin: V1d + the pinned tie-break.

**(c) Partial-bar REVERSE.**
- V1d applies the §10 ladder unchanged to the restored 4h: reverse flips it, omit drops it,
  discount halves it.
- The operator's 09-28 concern (un-hiding + REVERSE made the day more bullish) is real on
  that day, but it does not generalise. With no REVERSE on the restored 4h (c), holdout hit a
  falls 47.5% -> 44.1%. The study's V2 (a REVERSE needs both assets) is also worse (45.0 -> 44.1%).
- Pin: REVERSE unchanged. Any REVERSE change is out of scope.

**(d) P2-site suppression blanking P1 too.**
- By construction `suppressed_p2_sites ⊆ suppressed_p1_levels`
  (`_p2_nesting_suppression` requires `name in suppressed_p1_levels`). So "the P2-site also
  blanks P1" adds nothing beyond suppressed_p1. What the P2-site adds is blanking the P2 read.
- V1d lifts all three sets for the restored level, so its 4h can score as P2 (a meaningful
  candidate with an effective reject) or else as P1. P1 and P2 use the same points formula
  (`_BASE_POINTS * tf * tier * mag * reversal`) and the same sign. P1-only (d) changes 0
  calls on 685 dates.
- Pin: V1d (P2 allowed on the restored 4h). This is score-neutral. It needs a one-sentence
  exception in §2.1b's "no exception" paragraph.

**(e) Staleness and P4-supersedes-P3.**
- V1d ignores §2.1c staleness for the restored 4h: 1,112 of the 2,244 restorations are
  stale; 170 are stale-only. Never restoring a stale-only level (e) is equal within noise (25 calls change).
- V1d does not touch the mids at all. P4-supersedes-P3 and the "discounted 1h P4 must not
  supersede" hygiene fix (study V4: 1 flip, inert) are a different mechanism.
- Pin: V1d (the restored 4h is exempt from staleness). Mids and P4-supersedes-P3 are OUT of scope.

**(f) Session-level nesting and asymmetric nesting as an SMT signature.**
- V1d treats session-named levels like any other member of U. Only 6 of the 2,244 restored
  levels are session tier; the rest are promoted. Excluding session tier (f) is identical.
- The 09-28 `london(cur)_high` case is untouched. It has no mature 4h, and its SMT is lost to
  the P2 tier gate and the confirmation shape, not to nesting (brief, "Second case"). Using
  asymmetric cross-asset nesting to surface a divergence is a new rule. The closest measured
  variants are V5 (fresh session SMT, inert: 1 flip) and V6 (close-beyond London SMT, 46%,
  rejected).
- Pin: no special handling. OUT of scope.

**(g) New: determinism (found here).** Pinned in §1 step 4.

> ### 2.1g One standing 4h read per suppressed stack (per-timeframe level status)
>
> §2.1b nesting (incl. session nesting and most-extreme-swept-only shadowing), §2.1d
> duplicate collapsing, §2.1c equilibrium staleness and §2.1b P2/SMT nesting suppression
> exist so that ONE displacement is not scored once per stacked level. Applied level-wide
> and on every timeframe, they can also score it ZERO times: every level the move crossed
> is suppressed, and the move's standing higher-timeframe verdict vanishes from the sheet
> (2026-09-28 09:20 ET: MNQ had closed two 4h bars below `prev1_day_low`, `prev3_day_low`
> and `prev4_day_low` with no 4h close back; all were suppressed, and the sheet carried
> nothing from that 4h verdict). A suppressor collapses a stack to a representative; it
> must not collapse it to nothing.
>
> **Rule (code: `validate_contracts.per_tf_level_status`, exported by bench/facts.py as
> `level_tf_status`).** Per asset:
> 1. Take the suppressed set: `suppressed_p1_levels` ∪ `p1_stale_levels` ∪
>    `suppressed_p2_sites`, minus any live P2 site (a meaningful SMT candidate on that
>    asset not in `suppressed_p2_sites`, which already scores and is left untouched).
> 2. Per side (`*_high` / `*_low`, read from the level name; other names are never
>    restored), among the members with a completed 4h close since their sweep, restore the
>    MOST EXTREME one (highest high / lowest low). Ties: higher tier (week > day > session,
>    after §2.1f promotion), then facts level-map order.
> 3. The restored level scores on its **4h read only**, as an ordinary un-suppressed level:
>    P1, or P2 when it is a meaningful SMT candidate whose effective 4h verdict is a
>    rejection. Every multiplier and the §10 partial-bar ladder (discount / omit / reverse)
>    apply unchanged; §2.1c staleness does not. Its **1h read is retired** and scores
>    nothing. When the 1h agrees with the 4h this changes nothing (tf-dedup keeps the 4h).
>    When it has closed back through the level, the 4h verdict stands and the 1h recross is
>    what the §10 ladder already weighs.
> 4. Every other suppressed level stays suppressed on every timeframe. Un-suppressed
>    levels are untouched. DOL eligibility (`_dol_menu`), §3a near-maturity candidacy and the
>    S9 `mature_evidence_count` keep the level-wide sets.
>
> S9 tags the restored level's rows `[PER-TF: standing 4h read ...]` / `[PER-TF: 1h
> retired ...]`, and its SMT-candidate line `P2 LIVE ON 4h ONLY`.
>
> **Evidence (2026-09-29, 685 dates 2024-02..2026-09, ledger-only).** Directional hit
> (larger 09:30-13:00 excursion) 45.0% -> 48.0%; same days 45.4% -> 48.5%; 80 direct flips,

> anti-predictive ledger less so; it is not an edge claim. Alternatives measured and
> rejected: keeping the 1h (−0.9 pp), no REVERSE on the restored 4h (holdout −3.4 pp),

## 5. Code changes (file · function · change)

### 5.1 Data structure

New additive view key **`level_tf_status`**. It is SPARSE and lists only restored levels:

```python
level_tf_status = {
    "MNQ": {"prev3_day_low": {"1h": "retired", "4h": "live", "side": "low",
                              "was": ["nested", "stale", "p2_site"]}},
    "MES": {"prev3_week_high": {"1h": "retired", "4h": "live", "side": "high",
                                "was": ["nested", "stale"]}},
}
```

- `None` (key absent) or `{}`: exactly V0.
- A listed `(asset, level)`: the three legacy sets no longer apply to it. Each tf is `"live"`
  (P1/P2 eligible, staleness ignored) or `"retired"` (nothing scores on that tf).
- `was` is audit-only (reasons it was suppressed) and is NEVER read by scoring. Parity must
  not depend on it.
- The legacy sets `suppressed_p1_levels` / `p1_stale_levels` / `suppressed_p2_sites` stay
  exported **unchanged in content** in both flag states. They remain the source for the
  renderer, `_dol_menu`, `target.py`, near-maturity and tiebreak. `level_tf_status` is an
  override layer on top of them. This is backward compatible by construction.

### 5.2 Flag

`ACT_PER_TF_LEVEL_STATUS`, **default OFF**. On: `1/true/yes/on`. It is read in ONE place,
`agent/bench/facts.py::per_tf_level_status_enabled()`, modelled on
`tiebreak.tiebreak_enabled` but inverted: off unless explicitly on. `bundle_to_l1_view` is
the single builder for the online assembler and the offline bench, so the view is the only
seam. Rollback = unset the variable, or set it to `0`, and restart the process. The flag is
read at facts assembly, per call.

### 5.3 `agent/contracts/validate_contracts.py`

- **New** `per_tf_level_status(suppressed_p1_levels, p1_stale_levels, suppressed_p2_sites,
  level_htf_close_status, level_tiers, smt_candidates, p1_reasons=None) -> dict`: §1 steps
  1-5, verbatim, returning the §5.1 map. It is pure and total: `None`/missing inputs give
  `{}`. It is deterministic regardless of input list order and of `PYTHONHASHSEED`. Its
  local tier-rank dict must not import derive_facts. `p1_reasons` (`{asset: {name:
  [reason]}}`) only fills `was`; without it, `was` uses `"suppressed_p1"`/`"stale"`/`"p2_site"`.
- **`score_thesis_evidence(..., level_tf_status=None)`**: new kwarg, and a docstring paragraph.
  At function entry, when `level_tf_status` is truthy:
  - build LOCAL copies of the three sets with every listed `(asset, level)` removed. Never
    mutate the caller's dicts; the facts dict is shared across the four call sites;
  - build `_retired = {(asset, level, tf) for tf with status "retired"}`.
  - In the P1/P2 auto-injection loop (`for _tf3 in ("1h", "4h")`, ≈ :923), `continue` when
    `(_asset2, _name2, _tf3) in _retired`, before the `omit` check.
  - In the final zeroing loop (≈ :1250-1264), additionally zero any P1/P2 item whose
    `(asset, level, tf)` is in `_retired`. This is defence in depth: declared items at
    `level_tiers` pairs are already dropped.
  - Nothing else changes. That is the parity-by-construction argument: removing the level
    from the three sets plus skipping its 1h is exactly what `_v1` does by editing the
    kwargs. (`_v1` blanks `level_htf_close_status[a][n]["1h"]`; inside the scorer the only
    reader of a named level's status is that injection loop.)
- **`validate_thesis`** (≈ :176-219): read `facts.get("level_tf_status")` and pass it.
  If this step is skipped, the ARI_THESIS_BIAS re-check scores a different ledger from
  the real path (the 2026-07-20 bug class, thesis.md §10).

## 10. Risks

- **The study metric is not the production decision.** The study's call is sign(net) with a
  ±1 band. Production is: the model (constrained by the ledger outside the band), the
  tiebreak inside the band, the plan-37 stretch override bypassing the ledger on some days,
  and ledger_fallback on a validation failure. The live effect on NEUTRAL-band days is not
  what the study measured. §8.5 and A3 are the checks.
- **Call-site drift.** One missed scorer recreates the 2026-07-20 re-check divergence.
  Tests 21-22 guard it.
- **Hidden serialization of the vd** (§5.6) would break flag-off byte identity. The grep in
  Wave 2 and M4 guard it.
- **A model reading stale prompt text.** Under the flag before adoption, `_TASK_THESIS` still
  says P2-SUPPRESSED = nested / no evidence. The ledger is code-derived, so scoring is
  unaffected, but the model's narrative may contradict the S9 tags. That is acceptable
  because flag-on live use before adoption is not planned.
- **Tie-break reference drift.** If the study's `per_date.jsonl` is re-generated unpinned,
  the parity numbers wobble. Test 31 is anchored to `per_date_v1dp.jsonl`.
- **The live session is running on this machine.** Bundle builds, `-m slow` and cache
  extension (A3) only run outside session hours.

  overwritten, so the old keys stay readable. The rollback commit re-hits them.
