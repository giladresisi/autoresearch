# 36. Cross-asset SMT in the entry layer: a gate on §7, and a named-level two-bar reject

⚠️ Medium — the code is small; the prerequisites and the measurement are the work.

## Goal

Bring the MNQ/MES divergence below L1 for the first time. Two configurations came out of
the measurement, and **they are not two rules on one mechanism** — they sit on different
anchors:

| | anchor | accept test | SMT definition | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | running post-09:30 extreme | **R1** — the new-extreme bar closes WITH the thesis | MES did not make a new running extreme on that bar | **a gate on §7**, which already is anchor+R1 |
| **B** | a NAMED level (`ny_morning` extreme) | **R2** — the new-extreme bar closes AGAINST, the NEXT bar closes with | MES has not swept its own copy of that level | **a new mechanism** |

**R1's DEFINITION CHANGED on 2026-09-16 — see D3.** A new extreme now ARMS and the first
subsequent with-thesis close ENTERS. Row A above, and every R1/R2 number below, were
measured under the old same-bar definition and describe populations that no longer exist
separately. They are kept as the historical basis for the anchor split (running vs named),
which is unaffected; they are NOT the basis for the relaxed rule.

## D0 — MEASURE AT ENGINE LEVEL FIRST. Do not write A or B before this.

Every number above is signal-level: no attempt budget, no single-position rule, no T2
target, no `target_reached` death, and 3.4 fires per session against a 3-attempt budget.

**The precedent is exact and recent.** `fvg_1h_reject` fired 6 times in its signal scan and
**0 times in 29 engine sessions** — other mechanisms took the entry first, or the budget was
gone. A mechanism can be right in isolation and inert in the engine.

D0 is: replay N >= 29 sessions with the oracle direction injected (no model calls —
`run_replay(..., thesis=...)`), three arms — baseline, +A, +A+B — and compare realised
P&L, fires per mechanism, and attempts consumed. The existing driver is
`scratchpad/entries_scan.py`; fold it into `agent/study/` if it is to be kept.

**Stop rule:** if A fires fewer than ~1 time per 3 sessions in the engine, or if adding it
does not improve realised P&L, it does not ship regardless of the signal-level table.

## Prerequisites (both are design decisions, not typing)

**P1 — MES must reach the mechanism layer.** Nothing below L1 looks at MES today: the
Executor is MNQ-only (`self._ticker`, `_gaps_on`, every driver takes `mnq`). `bars` already
carries both, so this is plumbing, but it is a new capability and should be decided once,
for all mechanisms, not smuggled in per-mechanism.

**P2 — where named levels come from.** They exist in the facts (`liquidities`), but
`tmso_reject` and `fvg_1h_reject` both compute their own anchors from bars instead —
deliberately, to avoid re-keying the thesis cache. B needs `ny_morning` for BOTH assets.
Decide: compute from bars (consistent with the two existing mechanisms, no cache impact) or
consume the facts store (one source of truth, but MES levels are not built today — see
`build_menus`'s MNQ-only note). **Recommendation: compute from bars**, matching precedent.

## D2 — B: `ny_morning_reject`

A new market mechanism: MNQ's 1m bar sweeps the `ny_morning` extreme for the first time;
the bar closes against the thesis; the next bar closes with it; MES has not swept its own
`ny_morning` copy. Entry at the confirming bar's close, stop at the swept extreme -/+ 3
capped 15.

Reuses `reject_core` for the colour predicate and the capped stop. Registered like
`tmso_reject`: a market mechanism in `MarketMechanisms`, a self-gating class in
`planner.MECHANISM_CLASSES`.

## D3 — RELAXED R1: a new extreme ARMS, the first with-thesis close ENTERS

**Not a new mechanism.** §7 already has this shape and is already wired. D3 changes one
clause of its fire condition.

**Today's rule.** `ExtremeReject.on_bar_close` fires only when a bar is BOTH a new extreme
AND closes with the thesis. If the new-extreme bar closes adverse, the fire is lost for
good: the next bar is not a new extreme, so it only increments the quiet counter, and if no
further extreme prints the machine can never fire again that session.

**THE RULE AS OF 2026-09-16 (decided, not a candidate).** A new extreme ARMS the rejection;
entry is the FIRST subsequent 1m bar that closes with the thesis — the same bar when it
closes with, otherwise the first one after it. The extreme is the reference; the confirming
close is the trigger. This subsumes the old R1 (same-bar case) and the two-bar R2 case on
this anchor under one rule, which is why the two were never really two rules.

`QUIET_CLOSES_TO_ARM = 3` is UNCHANGED and stays. So does `STALE_EXTREME_AGE_HOURS`, the
track choice, and the stop policy.

**What must be pinned when this is built** — each is a rule-level decision, not typing:

- **The pending window is CAPPED.** "First with-thesis close" without a bound is the same
  unbounded-wait defect the 07:00-FVG veto was rejected for. Pick N bars (start at 3, the
  same order as the arm counter), measure, and record the number.
- **A newer extreme supersedes.** While pending, a new extreme updates the reference and
  RESTARTS the window. It does not cancel the pending state.
- **The stop anchors to the SWEPT EXTREME, not the confirming bar's wick** — extreme -/+ 3,
  capped at `SL_CAP_PTS` 15, matching the policy every signal-level number in this plan was
  measured under. The current `_fire` anchors on the firing bar's opposite wick; on a
  later confirming bar that is a different and looser stop.
- **The quiet counter while pending.** Decide explicitly whether a pending bar that closes
  adverse counts as quiet. Recommendation: yes, unchanged — arming and confirming are
  independent.

**Verified consequences, both hand-read against the tape:**

```

  09:39  L29059.75  C29072.00  green  NEWLOW  -> fires TODAY too. Entry 09:40:00, -15.00.
  09:41  L29040.00  C29051.50  RED    NEWLOW  -> new extreme, adverse close -> PENDING

**So relaxed R1 DOES produce 09-01's winning entry**, which neither A nor B could. The
09:39 loser still fires first — D3 re-times nothing about it — so 09-01 needs two attempts,

```

**But it does NOT fire there, and that is correct behaviour we are keeping.** A new-extreme
tick resets `_quiet` to 0 whatever the close colour, and 09:30 and 09:31 are both new
extremes on the since-arm track, so at 09:32 the machine has `quiet=0` and is not armed.
`QUIET_CLOSES_TO_ARM = 3` cannot be satisfied before ~09:34 on this day. **08-31 still needs
D2 (the named-level anchor); relaxed R1 does not reach it.**

**THE MEASUREMENT TABLE ABOVE NO LONGER DESCRIBES THIS RULE.** R1 (186 fires, 21% survive)
and R2 (72 fires) were measured as disjoint populations on the running-extreme anchor.
Relaxed R1 is their union under a capped window, which is neither row, and D1's SMT pairing
(`R1 smt=True` +41.00/fire vs `R2 smt=True` +19.60/fire) was the argument for gating A on
SMT at all. **D0 must re-measure the relaxed rule, with and without the SMT gate, before
D1 is written** — the inversion that motivated A may not survive the merge.

**FALSE ENTRIES ARE THE RISK.** Relaxed R1 fires at least as often as R1 alone — 186 fires
over 92 sessions, ~2 per session against a 3-attempt budget — plus every two-bar case it
now picks up. This is the one deliverable here that can only ADD fires. The capped window
is the sole brake, so D0's stop rule applies to it with full force: if the relaxed rule does
not improve realised engine P&L, the cap is wrong or the rule is.

**Test dates, hand-read and available:** 09-01 (must produce 09:43:00; the 09:39 fire must

**Under B the budget is no longer the binding constraint on the days this plan targets:**

- **08-31** — first fill 09:40:06, so at 09:33:00 the budget is untouched, 0 of 3. B's

Caveat, and it is not small: n=5, on the five days this whole cycle has been fitted to, and
08-31 is a genuine counterexample (B deletes the day's only winner). **The 29-session
engine scan is a prerequisite, not a formality.**

## Out of scope

- **Generalising the sweep-then-reject vocabulary.** Deferred deliberately: too many
  nuances across §6, §7, `tmso_reject`, `fvg_1h_reject` and B, and the existing ones have
  not yet earned it. `reject_core` stays a two-function helper and is NOT extended into a
  shared machine here.
- **Refactoring §6 or §7 beyond D1's flag.** Their constants trace to recorded A/Bs and
  they have no named-case coverage; a refactor is currently unfalsifiable.
- The 07:00-FVG suppression veto (base rate 3-in-94, two resolving instantly).
- Pooled R1+R2+SMT (measured worse than no filter).
- Any timing or budget gate. Three were measured and refuted: the fast-stop-out penalty (a
  fast stop-out predicts a BETTER next entry — 28% vs 18%), attempt caps (attempts 2-3 are
  worth +661.50 over 29 sessions), and stretch-conditioned early suppression. The fourth —
  unarming the two 5m-FVG entry mechanisms — measured positive and ships as its OWN commit,
  outside this plan. This plan assumes it is in force (see the budget finding above); if it
  is reverted, 09-01's and 08-31's entries become unaffordable again.
