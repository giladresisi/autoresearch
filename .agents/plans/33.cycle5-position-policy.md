## 1. The discipline this plan must hold

**The policy is causal.** Every input it reads is knowable when it acts: the mechanism's own
stop, FVG completions, a close beyond the initial target, a 3-pt opposite bar, a DOL touch, the
clock. Lookahead enters ONLY in scoring. If any clause ends up needing a future value, that is a
defect to report, not to work around.

**The comparison set is fixed before any result is seen** (§6). No baseline may be added,
dropped or re-parameterised after the numbers exist.

**2026-09-02 generated this rule.** Its result is an acceptance test, never evidence. The other
83 sessions are the evidence. Any summary that pools 09-02 into a corpus statistic is wrong.

**Discovery optimism on this corpus is ~15 points** (cycle 4, measured). Nothing here is fitted,
so that figure is a caution rather than a correction — but if a parameter *does* get tuned, it
must be declared and scored out-of-sample.

## 2. The policy, clause by clause

Stated for a LONG; **every clause mirrors for a short** (adverse = up, `bar1.Low` for bear gaps).

| # | clause |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Entry**: a real Executor fill — time, price, direction, and the mechanism's own initial stop. **One position per session, the FIRST fill. No re-entry** — that is the entry mechanisms' business. The Executor emits the fill event; the policy simulator owns everything after. |
| 2 | **Initial stop**: whatever the entry mechanism outputs. Do not invent one, do not cap it further. |
| 3 | **BE trigger**: the first new direction-matching 1m continuation FVG **completing** after the fill. Stop → **the fill price exactly**. |
| 4 | **BE fallback**: if BE has not fired when price has covered **50% of the fill→DOL distance**, move to BE then. |
| 5 | **Trail**: on each subsequent FVG completion, stop → **3 pts beyond the far (adverse) edge of the PREVIOUS FVG** (long: 3 pts below the previous bull gap's `bar1.High`), so price must traverse both gaps to reach it. |
| 6 | **Regime flip**: the first 1m bar **closing beyond** the initial target. The comparison is against the **offset-adjusted published price** (`cautious_price_initial`, e.g. 29147.25), never the raw level — that is what production's ladder emits and acts on, and it needs no second lookup. Settled per §4 D4. |
| 7 | **Post-flip**: on each 1m bar closing opposite by **≥3 pts**, stop → 3 pts beyond that bar's adverse wick extreme. **Both §5 and §7 remain active; take the tighter.** |
| 8 | **Monotone**: the stop never loosens. Any clause proposing a looser stop is a no-op. |
| 9 | **DOL**: market close on **touch**. |
| 10 | **13:00 ET**: market close if still open. |
| 11 | Actions execute at the **open of the next 1m bar** after the triggering close. |

### 2.1 The input contract — both targets are INJECTED

**This module never selects a target and its logic never depends on how one was selected.** It
receives two prices from upstream and is indifferent to their provenance:

- the **DOL** (primary target), and
- the **initial target**, supplied by the target-selection module — currently production's
  `compute_cautious_prices` at the fill.

That indifference is deliberate: it is what lets target-layer work (the fill re-anchor, the
fill-time draw floor) proceed in parallel without touching this plan. But the module still owes a
stated contract for the values it is handed, and these are it:

| # | contract clause |
|---|---|
| 12 | **Initial target ABSENT** (`compute_cautious_prices` returns empty — no candidate inside its caps): **no regime flip ever occurs**. Clauses 3, 5 and 8 continue unchanged until the DOL touch or 13:00. Clause 7 is inert for that session. |
| 13 | **Initial target BEYOND the DOL**: **ignored entirely — treated exactly as absent**, per clause 12. It is not clamped and not relocated. |
| 14 | **DOL absent**: no entry at all, reproducing the contract-layer gate explicitly (§4 (a)). |

**Clauses 12 and 13 must be counted, not merely handled.** How often each fires is a result: a
session where the post-flip ratchet never arms is a session where half the policy is inert, and
that rate belongs in the report alongside capture and scratch rate.

**FVG definition** is the codebase's, not a new one — `agent/facts/detectors/fvg.py`: bull gap
`bar3.Low > bar1.High` spanning `[bar1.High, bar3.Low]`; three bars adjacent on the tf grid and
inside one CME session; **completes at bar 3**.

**Minimum FVG height is a pre-registered variant, not a fixed choice** (§6). The default is **no
filter**; a minimum-height variant runs alongside it. The 10:13 gap that anchors a stop on Sep 2
is **1.25 pts wide**, so a no-filter rule lets trivial imbalances set stops — the direct
mechanism behind the "ratchet converges too fast" risk.

**Stops are touch-based**, adverse same-bar resolution, per plan 31.

## 4. The four items, resolved

### (a) The no-DOL path — verified in code

Production's "no target → no entry" is enforced **at the thesis contract layer, not in the
Executor**:

- `agent/contracts/validate_contracts.py:98` — *"directional thesis requires dol.level and
  dol.price"*. An empty menu therefore yields NEUTRAL → no plan → no arming → no entry.
- `agent/trader/executor.py:448` — `_dol_price()` returns `None` when the plan carries no DOL.
- `agent/trader/executor.py:1183` — `if dol is not None: … if remaining < DOL_FLOOR_PTS: veto`.
  **A `None` DOL SKIPS the veto**, so entry proceeds.
- `:465` — `dol_reached` death is skipped too; `:112` — `OrderSim(dol=None)` has no take-profit.

**Because we supply DOLs synthetically and bypass L1, we bypass the real gate.** Resolution:
**reproduce it explicitly** — a session with no DOL on a track produces **no position on that
track**, asserted by a test, never inherited from the Executor's silence.

### (b) Track divergence — report both sets, never a pooled mean

Sessions where production's D1 is absent get no position on that track but may still have an
oracle DOL. A mean across different session sets is meaningless. **Every result is reported on
(i) the common subset and (ii) each track's own set, with counts.** Sep 2 is itself a divergent
session (D3), so this is load-bearing from the first run.

### (c) Bar resolution — 1m logic, 1s touches, and re-run plan 31's baselines

The rule is 1m (FVG completion, closes, the 3-pt bar); stops are touch-based; production replays
1s. **Resolution: 1m for logic, 1s for touch evaluation** — production's own "bar time inside the
loop" shape. **Consequence: plan 31's B-tight/B-loose were computed on 1m**, so they must be
re-run on the same 1m-logic/1s-touch basis or the comparison drifts. Task 6 does this; plan 31 §7
gives way to the re-run values, which supersede §4 for this plan only and must be recorded as
such.

Note from the re-entry study: 1m with adverse-first resolution is a **conservative** upper bound
on stop firing; 1s only refines it downward.

### (d) The handoff boundary

**The Executor emits the fill event only** — `(time, price, direction, initial_stop,
mechanism_id)`. The policy simulator owns every subsequent decision. `OrderSim`'s own stop and
DOL take-profit are **not** used; two owners of one stop is a defect waiting to happen. Asserted
by a test that the simulator never reads `OrderSim.position`.

## 9. RESULT (2026-09-08) — what this measured is the ENTRY's stop, not the management rule

### Read this before the capture table

**The policy did not get to act on most of the corpus, so most of what follows is a
measurement of the entry mechanism's stop placement rather than of the management rule this
plan set out to test.**

Decomposing the 58 stop exits on the production track by which clause set the stop that
actually fired:

| what set the firing stop | sessions |
|---|---|
| **the entry mechanism's own initial stop (clause 2)** | **31** |
| clause 3 — break-even | 16 |
| clause 4 — the BE fallback | 1 |
| clause 5 — the FVG trail | 1 |
| clause 7 — the post-flip ratchet | 9 |

**The trail — the part of the policy this plan exists to test — sets the firing stop on 10
of 66 sessions.** On 31 of 66 the position was dead before a single clause fired. Median
time to the policy's first action is **3.0 minutes**; median time to exit is **1.3
minutes**; on 31 sessions the first move would have arrived after the position had already
closed.

Every number below is therefore a joint measurement of two things, and this corpus cannot
separate them. Read the capture figures as "what this policy achieved ON TOP OF these
entries", never as "what this policy is worth".

Hand-checked, 2026-05-04: long filled 28145.50 at 09:36:52 with the mechanism's 11.0-pt stop
at 28134.50; the tape reaches 28134.00 at **09:37:06**, fourteen seconds later. The first
continuation FVG does not complete until **09:43:00**. There is no defect here — the policy
is simply slower than the trade.

**This is a defect in the GATE'S DESIGN, not in its execution.** Task 1 was specified to
gate on fill yield and post-fill room; both were measured, both were healthy (78.6% and a
205-pt median), and both were reported before any capture number, exactly as §8 requires.
Neither quantity can detect that a filled position dies in 78 seconds. **The gate a study in
this shape needs is SURVIVAL — the fraction of fills that live long enough for the rule under
test to act — and the next plan of this kind should gate on that instead.**

### Then the negative

On the production DOL track, over 66 sessions with real Executor fills, the policy captures
**8.80%** of the oracle ceiling (95% session-clustered CI **[3.03%, 14.91%]**).
Pre-registered B-tight captures **7.52%** on the same fills, so the policy beats it by **1.3
points of capture** — inside its own interval. It **loses to B-loose** (11.63%).

Plan 31 §5 records B-tight as scratch-prone and cheap to beat. **§8's first failure
condition is met: the two-gap buffer does not do what it was designed to do.** That is the
verdict and it is not a tuning prompt.

**And every clause bolted around the trail is worse than nothing.** A 2x2 ablation — reported
arms, not §6 baselines — finds that removing the regime clauses (6, 7) AND the breakeven pair
(3, 4) both help, and compose: the bare lagged trail reaches **0.1065** on production and
**0.2331** on oracle. That last arm is also the only configuration in this study that beats
B-loose on either track, and it beats B-tight by +0.0313 / +0.1342 — so **the user's two-gap
buffer does what it was designed to do, and what fails is everything added around it.**

Two caveats travel with that, both below: the loss-tail guard fires (removing breakeven makes
more sessions worse than better on production and still nets positive, so the mean rests on a
few large wins), and **arm D beats an unmanaged hold only on the ORACLE track** — §8's second
failure condition, which arm A escaped only because it exited before its target mattered.

### The tail: FATTER, not DEEPER — and that is the finding the guard produced

Removing breakeven adds **11 losing sessions on production (31 → 42)** and **4 on oracle
(18 → 22)**, worsening total losses by **−253.9** and **−93.8**. On capture alone that would
be invisible.

**But the worst single session is −35.8 on ALL FOUR ARMS, to the cent.** The left tail gets
fatter and does not get deeper — and the reason is structural: the entry mechanism's own stop
already caps the worst case, so breakeven cannot prevent a large loss because there are no
large losses for it to prevent. What it actually does on this corpus is convert small losses
into exact scratches, and it pays for that by clipping winners. Production: removing it costs
253.9 points on the loss side and gains 384.3 on the winning side, netting +130.4.

**So the honest verdict on breakeven is not "remove it" — it is "on this corpus it is buying
something that is already paid for".** The 25-pt stop cap is doing the loss-limiting work.

**Which gives the coupling its own claim: breakeven's value is a FUNCTION OF THE INITIAL
STOP'S WIDTH.** At `STOP_CAP_PTS = 25` there are no large losses for breakeven to prevent, so
it can only convert small losses into scratches and pay for that by clipping winners — which
is precisely what the arithmetic above shows. At a materially wider stop there WOULD be real
losses to prevent, the loss side would carry more weight, and the arithmetic could invert.

**So the breakeven question is not decidable independently of the entry-stop question.** That
is the same constraint that dominates this entire plan — the opening section reaches it from
survival (positions die on the mechanism's stop before any clause fires), and the tail reaches
it from loss prevention (the cap has already prevented the losses breakeven exists for). Two
independent routes to the same place.

**It is therefore the strongest argument in this document for where the next cycle goes: the
initial stop.** Plan 31 §6 already records `STOP_CAP_PTS = 25.0` as a POINTS constant against
an `avg_range_1h` that ranged 52.40-95.76 on this corpus — 0.26x to 0.48x, nearly a factor of
two — and left it deliberately unfixed because the baseline's job was to reproduce production.
Nothing downstream of the entry can be evaluated cleanly until that number is either defended
or replaced.

### The STOP-WIDTH SWEEP — PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS, recorded before the run

The 2x2 above concluded that breakeven's value is a function of the initial stop's width, and
that nothing downstream of the entry can be evaluated cleanly until that constant is defended
or replaced. This sweep tests that directly. **The predictions are the team lead's, written
down before the numbers existed.**

**Widths.** Control is the mechanism's own stop, unchanged, so it must reproduce the existing
numbers to the cent. Then fixed widths from the FILL price at 50 pts and at 0.7 / 1.0 / 1.3 /

**A second variable rides along, and it is named rather than hidden.** Production's stop is a
STRUCTURAL level (the bound gap's far edge plus a buffer) CAPPED at 25 pts. A fixed width
therefore removes the structural anchoring *as well as* widening. A `max(structural, width)`
variant separates the two — it keeps the gap-derived anchor and only refuses to be tighter
than the width — and it needs the UNCAPPED structural stop, which the entry feed now records
alongside the capped one and checks against it.

**Arms.** A (full), C (no breakeven), D (pure trail) at every width. A-vs-C is the breakeven
axis; D is there because it is the best configuration and the question is whether a wider stop
lets it clear the bar it currently fails.

width.** Both are defined from the initial stop — B-loose *is* the initial stop, never moved.
Comparing a wide-stop policy against a 25-pt-stop baseline would hand the policy a win it did
not earn, and the error would be invisible in the output.

**Risk-adjusted, not only capture.** A 1.6 x avg_1h stop risks roughly six times a 25-pt stop,
so raw capture ranks the widest arm first by construction. Points captured per point risked is
reported beside capture throughout.

**The three predictions:**

1. **`loss_worst` deepens as the stop widens.** It is −35.75 on all four arms today because
   the 25-pt cap clips it. **If it does not move, the stop is not the binding constraint and
   the whole diagnosis is wrong** — and that is the headline if it happens.
2. **A-vs-C flips sign at some width.** Breakeven has nothing to prevent at 25 pts; as real
   losses appear it should begin to earn its place. **If it never flips at any width, breakeven

3. **Survival improves.** Median time to exit, the fraction dying before any clause fires, and
   the fraction dying on the initial stop — 1.3 min, 31/66 and 31/58 at the control — should

trailing does not beat leaving the stop alone at achievable target quality.

**What the sweep cannot say.** Every arm uses the SAME fills. This is a counterfactual on RISK,
not on entries: it says what these entries do at different stop widths, and it says nothing
about what a different entry mechanism would produce. A wider stop in production would also
change which candidates pass the DOL-floor veto and the max-distance guard, and none of that is
modelled here.

### What the incumbent actually does — both semantics questions, settled from the code

Two things had to be pinned before any legacy-ladder number could mean anything. Both are
settled, and both come out against the model the task assumed.

**1. The cautious rungs are NOT take-profit levels. They ARM A TRAILING STOP.** `trend.py` is
the cautious-mode manager and reaching a rung never exits the position:

* initial rung surpassed with a CLOSE beyond it, and only when the rung is at least
  `INITIAL_STOP_MIN_DIST_PTS = 50` from the fill → arm a stop at the last same-direction **5m**
  reference bar's body bound, floored at fill ± `BREAKEVEN_BUFFER_PTS = 5.0`
  (`trend.py:524-540`);
* initial rung surpassed WICK-ONLY → stop to the **midpoint** between the original stop and the
  initial target (`:541-552`);
* secondary rung surpassed with a close beyond → arm or move the stop off the last
  same-direction **1m** reference bar (`:506-522`, `:630-643`);
* once armed it **trails every 5m**, tighten-only, off the newest same-direction 5m reference
  bar (`:649-664`);
* the position leaves on the stop being broken, on a daily-mid or weekly-mid cross
  market-close, or at session end.

`SECONDARY_TP_ON_TOUCH_0930` — the only take-profit-on-touch in the file — is **`False` by
default** and window-limited to 09:30-09:45 (`:80`).

**So "a single full exit at the secondary" is not the incumbent, and neither is a scale-out at
the initial rung.** Bracketing those two would bracket two things that are both wrong.

**2. Yes, the legacy engine manages its stop — extensively.** Breakeven-floored arming, a
midpoint stop on a wick touch, and a tighten-only 5m structural trail. **An incumbent modelled
as "static stop plus two take-profits" would understate it substantially.**

**Consequence for this plan.** The incumbent is a *structural trailing system* — much closer to
this plan's arm D than to a take-profit ladder — and the honest comparison is therefore between
two trails that differ in their anchor: the legacy engine trails off same-direction **reference
bars** (5m body bounds, floored at breakeven), while clause 5 trails off the **previous FVG's
far edge**. That is a sharper and more interesting question than the one this section was
opened to answer, and it is NOT answered here: building it faithfully needs `trend.py`'s own
`_last_same_dir_ref_bar` and `_floored_break_price` (importable), the daily and weekly mids
(available from `legacy_liquidities.reconstruct`, which already emits `day_mid` / `week_mid`),
the session ATH, and the position/hypothesis state machine that carries `cautious` state across
bars. **No legacy-ladder number is reported in this plan, deliberately** — a number produced
under the wrong model of the incumbent would be worse than none.

### Why the ladder still fails — three causes, all DOCUMENTED deviations now shown not to be harmless

(The fourth and largest cause of the first reading — contract routing — was mine and is fixed.
It is recorded rather than quietly corrected, because it is exactly the class of error this
gate exists to catch: a 293.25-point offset looked at first like a deep modelling failure when
it was a one-line path bug.)

1. **`dist_shrinks` is unknowable and it changes the selection.** The per-position
   `cautious_dist_shrinks` counter shrinks both caps by 15% per failed entry and lives in
   `position.json`. Demonstrated on 2026-05-18 09:50: at `dist_shrinks` 0 or 1 the ladder
   selects a real level (29215.25); at 2 the pool empties and it falls through to
   `synthetic_85pct` — which is exactly what the engine recorded. 05-18 had **21 stop-outs**,
   so the counter was certainly non-zero for most episodes, and it cannot be recovered from
   the event stream.
2. **`invalidated_names=None` makes the pool an UPPER BOUND.** Levels the engine had retired
   (GIL-25's per-ticker depletion latch, in `smts.json`) are still candidates here, so the
   study can select a rung the engine no longer had.
3. **List ORDER decides ties, and the order is not reconstructible.** On 2026-05-18 09:50
   `asia_low`, `day_low` and `week_low` all sit at 29217.25; `compute_cautious_prices` uses
   `max(..., key=price)`, which returns the FIRST maximal element in iteration order. The
   engine's `liquidities` order is whatever `run_daily_fixed` plus a session's worth of
   `_set` appends produced; `legacy_liquidities.reconstruct` emits its own fixed order. Tied
   levels therefore resolve to different NAMES even where the price agrees.

A fourth, narrower one: most of these episodes are overnight fills (19:15, 22:11, 03:25,
03:47, 07:32), and `reconstruct` passes `today = now.date()` to `_session_bars`, where
`session_pipeline` uses `now.date() + 1 day` for the Asia block between 18:00 and midnight.
The 2026-08-25 validation was a single RTH instant and could not see this.

### What this scopes, and it reaches back into this plan

**The same reconstruction supplies this plan's INITIAL TARGET** — clause 6's flip price, and
therefore the whole regime half of the policy. What the 08-25 check validated was the
liquidity LIST at one RTH instant, exactly. What this gate shows is that the LADDER SELECTED
from that list is sensitive to two inputs that cannot be reconstructed at all
(`dist_shrinks`, `invalidated_names`) and one that cannot be reconstructed reliably (list
order under ties).

So the initial-target figures in this plan should be read as **a plausible ladder, not the
ladder the engine would have had** — with the qualification that the ladder now measures as
roughly half right on names (8 of 17) rather than wholly wrong, and that the machinery
consuming it is correct. Three things are unaffected and worth separating out:

* the 2026-09-02 fixture still reproduces plan 33 §3's independently derived 29147.25 /
  `ny_morning_high` exactly — one instant where the reconstruction demonstrably agrees;
* the 08-25 liquidity-list validation stands as what it was, a list check at one instant;
* **the clause-6/7 results are unaffected in direction**, and there is a tighter bound
  available than "a net-negative half does not become positive with a better target".

  **Arm D is the LIMIT of arm C as the initial target recedes.** Clause 6 flips on the first
  1m close beyond the initial target, so a farther target flips later and fires clause 7 later
  or never; in the limit — a target at or beyond the DOL, or absent — clauses 12/13 make the
  regime half inert, which is exactly arm D. A nearer target flips earlier and fires clause 7
  more, which is the direction measured worse. **So across the whole space of initial targets,
  C's behaviour is bounded above by D**, and no reconstruction error in the target can put C
  past that ceiling. D is measured, and it is measured on a target-independent derivation.

  **The assumption that bound rests on, stated rather than buried:** that clause 7's firings
  are net-harmful *at the margin*. That is what was measured (removing them helped on both
  tracks) but it is not logically necessary — a clamp landing just before a genuine reversal
  helps, and a better-chosen target could in principle select FOR the helpful firings rather
  than merely producing fewer of them. That possibility is unmeasured here and the bound
  should not be leaned on as arithmetic.

**No legacy-ladder or legacy-trail number appears in this plan.** Producing one under a
ladder that reproduces 3 of 39 recorded stop prices and 8 of 17 rung names would have been
worse than none. The arm stops here: recovering the missing inputs means replaying the legacy
engine with state capture, which writes `daily.json` and `global.json` — the write class the
standing constraints forbid, and one this repo has a documented instance of escaping its
isolation. The gate's verdict is the arm's outcome.

**And this is an argument FOR the target change already ordered, not only a limitation.** The
initial target currently rests on a reconstruction that depends on `cautious_dist_shrinks`,
the depletion set, and the liquidity list's iteration order — **none of which can be recovered
from bars, in principle rather than for want of effort.** Deriving it instead from the primary
target moves it onto a derivation that is ours: deterministic, reproducible, and checkable
without a legacy replay. A study input that cannot be reconstructed is a permanent ceiling on
what any number built from it can claim, and replacing it removes that ceiling rather than
merely working around it.

That closes the arc this plan has been tracing. Removing clauses 6/7 helped; removing 3/4
helped; D — the bare lagged trail — beat every richer arm; and the incumbent, which arms on
25 of 66 sessions, edges D. **On this corpus, at this stop width, the ranking of management
rules is very nearly the reverse of how much they intervene.** Which is the same finding the
survival section reached from the other end: the entry's stop, not the management rule, is
what bounds this system.

The pruned pool sharpens it — arming even less often (0.56 moves), carrying MORE losing
sessions (48-49 against 41) and still scoring higher, because the winners it does not clip
are large.

### How this arm was decided, recorded with the data-layer bugs

The bracket ran only because a message crossing beat its own withdrawal. That is luck, and the
decisions around it are worth recording beside the two wrong-source bugs, because they are the
same failure one layer up.

**Twice on this thread a call was made by reasoning about what a measurement COULD show rather
than letting it show it** — first "abandon the arm as unreachable", then "withdraw the bracket
as unable to license anything". Both would have discarded a result that turned out to be the
cycle's clearest. **Cheap measurement beats confident reasoning about measurement**, and the
tell in both cases was *an argument that a number cannot be informative, offered before the
number exists.*

**But the second call was less wrong than its own retraction made it.** At one and a half of
three inputs swept, the original objection — that the bracket could not license a claim about
the incumbent — was closer to right than the retraction that replaced it. **And the run's value
never rested on the sweep at all:** the decisive output was the NO-MANAGEMENT comparison
(B-loose at 0.1315 beating everything, the legacy trail arming on only 25 of 66), and neither
of those depends on the sweep's coverage. So the honest entry is both halves at once — **the
bracket licensed less than either party claimed at its most generous, and the run was worth
running for something the bracket was not designed to find.** Recording only the first half
would indict the method; only the second would flatter it.

**The pairing matters more than either instance.** One side reported a diagnosis twice before
checking its input — "the ladder is wholly wrong", "the residual is reference-bar selection" —
and both times the cause was a data source. The other side argued twice that a measurement
could not inform, before letting it run. **Neither was short of reasoning; both were short of a
cheap check.** The data-layer version is the two wrong-source bugs, where a contract-scale
offset and a bar-provenance tick each presented as a deep modelling failure. Same shortfall,
different layers, and the pairing is the generalisation worth keeping — the individual
instances are not.

**One correction against my own favour.** The withdrawal was retracted partly on the grounds
that the sweep covered two of the three unrecoverable inputs rather than one and a half,
crediting the pruned pool as a proxy for the depletion latch. **It is a weaker proxy than that
credit implies.** GIL-25's latch retires a level whose resting liquidity is DEPLETED — a
specific consuming event — whereas the pruned pool drops any level price merely traded through
THIS session. It is therefore broader in one direction (touched-but-not-depleted levels are
dropped) and narrower in another (levels depleted on a PRIOR session stay in the pool). It is a
real second dimension and it does bound, but it is not the latch, and "one and a half of three"
remains the more accurate count.

### What the bracket does and does not settle

**Settles LESS than a bracket appears to.** The ladder has THREE unrecoverable inputs and this
sweeps one and a half of them: `cautious_dist_shrinks` is genuinely swept; the depletion set is
crudely bounded (full pool against an aggressive prune); and **list iteration order is not a
parameter at all** — it is whatever `run_daily_fixed` plus a session of appends happened to
produce, with no range to sweep over. A verdict stable across this sweep therefore says our
reconstruction's verdict is insensitive to ONE parameter. **It does not license any claim about
what the incumbent would have done**, and a table that looks like a bounded comparison while
being a partial one is the same failure mode as the ladder number that was refused, one level
up.

**Does not settle:** whether these are the stops the engine would actually have placed. The
schedule still does not reproduce, and the bracket sweeps the ladder's INPUTS without fixing
its list-order tie-breaking or the overnight session-window handling. And every arm here uses
the Executor's fills, which are not the legacy engine's own — **this cannot say what the
legacy system would have produced end-to-end.** It answers "given these entries, does the
cautious trail or an FVG trail manage them better", and nothing wider.

### Synthesis across two studies: widen and decay; never clamp on an event

**This is not a plan-33 result.** It reconciles this plan's ablation with cycle 4's
time-conditioned trail, and it is stated here because the two look contradictory and a
reader who meets them separately will not be able to square them.

| | what it found |
|---|---|
| cycle 4, time-conditioned trail | **tightening BEAT a fixed trail** — `D = D0 + k·√(elapsed min)`, `D0 = 1.60`, `k = −0.100`, LOSO +0.0705 over the best fixed D |
| plan 33, clause-12 ablation | **tightening COST points** — clause 7 turned 8 oracle DOL touches into stop-outs and lifted the scratch rate 0.683 → 0.878 |

Both are about tightening a stop as a move matures, and they disagree. The reconciliation is
**how** the tightening happens, not whether:

- **Gradual decay from a wide base helps.** The time-conditioned trail starts at
  1.60 × avg_1h — wide — and contracts smoothly with elapsed time. It is never tight early
  and never tightens abruptly.
- **Sudden event-triggered clamping to a tight level hurts.** Clause 7 jumps the stop to
  3 pts beyond a single bar's wick, at the moment the initial target clears. That is both
  tight in absolute terms and discontinuous in time.

This is consistent with the exposure finding that a move's absolute pullback tolerance
**grows** as it matures, because later stages occupy more wall-clock time and therefore more
noise. A stop that clamps tight immediately after an event is then taken out by ordinary
late-move noise — which is exactly the mechanism the oracle track's 8 lost DOL touches show.

**The rule this suggests: widen and decay; never clamp on an event.**

**Cross-basis caveat, and it is not a footnote.** The time-conditioning figure is
ORIGIN-ANCHORED — measured from the segment origin, where the position starts maximally in
profit — and **has never been measured on real Executor fills**. The ablation here is on real
fills. So the synthesis is a hypothesis about the SHAPE of a good trail, supported from two
directions, and not a quantitative claim that either number transfers to the other's basis.
Testing the time-conditioned trail on real fills is the obvious next measurement and this
plan does not make it.

Like the ablation itself, this is a CANDIDATE with evidence. By step 0 of the change protocol
it belongs in `l2-mechanisms.md` §11, not in §§2-8, and that document is untouched by this
plan.

### What these numbers are NOT

**They are not a verdict on the policy's clauses.** Nearly half the corpus never reaches
clause 3, and the trail sets the firing stop on 10 of 66 sessions. What has been measured is
overwhelmingly the **entry mechanism's stop placement**, not the management rule on top of
it. This corpus cannot separate the two, because the median position does not survive long
enough for the separation to exist.

**Clause 1 is load-bearing in a way the plan did not anticipate.** "One position per session,
the FIRST fill. No re-entry" is what makes a 1.3-minute median hold the whole of a session's
result. Production would take up to three attempts (`MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3`); the study takes one
and stops. Whether the policy is any good on a *surviving* attempt is a different question
from the one this plan answered.

**The Task-1 gate measured the wrong quantity, and it passed** — see the opening section.
The gate was specified on yield and room; neither can see that a filled position dies in 78
seconds. That is a lesson about how to specify the gate, not about how this one was run.

**Two of the three external references are not reproducible from this repository.** The
0.4665 and 0.5370 LOSO capture figures appear nowhere outside §6 of this plan, so their fill
convention and their capture denominator could not be checked. If they were measured from an
oracle fill, or without production's 25-pt initial stop, they are not commensurable with a
figure computed from real mechanism fills, and the comparison against them says less than
the table implies. Recorded rather than adjusted: §6 is frozen.

**Nothing here was fitted.** No parameter was tuned, so cycle 4's ~15-point discovery
optimism is a caution rather than a correction. The height variant was pre-registered and is
reported whichever way it went.

### Two defects found in review, fixed, and what they changed

Both were found by the post-execution code review, after a first set of numbers had already
been produced. The numbers above are from the corrected run; the first set is superseded.

1. **Clause 8's monotone fold was seeded with nothing instead of with the mechanism's stop.**
   A regime flip needs no FVG, so clause 7 can be the first proposal, and an unseeded fold
   accepted it unconditionally — letting a wick-anchored ratchet move the stop BELOW clause
   2's. Clause 8 and clause 2 violated together, silently. Fixed at
   `position_policy._monotone`; pinned by `test_clause_7_can_never_loosen_the_mechanisms_own_stop`.
   Effect: production capture 0.0780 → 0.0880.
2. **The 1s partial bar carried cumulative extremes where the backtest carries raw
   per-second ones** (`backtest_smt.py:1690`). The Executor reads that row for `now_mid` —
   §11's calibrated market-fill price — and for the crossed-trigger test, so every market
   fill was priced on a basis the mechanisms were never calibrated against. Fixed in
   `entries._replay_frames`; pinned by
   `test_the_partial_minute_carries_RAW_per_second_extremes_for_mnq`. Effect: the fill moved
   on 17 of 168 session-tracks and the outcome category on 2; yield 77.4% → 78.6%.

Neither changed the direction of the verdict, and saying so is not a defence of the first
run — it is the reason both are recorded here rather than quietly replaced.

### Divergences from the plan as written

1. **The entry DOL is `build_menus` D1 at the 09:20 L1 boundary, not at the fill.** §2.1's
   "at the fill" is circular for an entry feed — the fill cannot exist until the Executor
   has a DOL, since that is what its `dol_floor` veto and `dol_reached` death are computed
   against. It is also not what production does (`target_offline.py`: the DOL is taken once
   at 09:20 and nothing before the fill reconsiders it). `targets_for_policy` implements and
   tests D1-at-the-fill as a separate field for the target layer, but the corpus run does
   not carry it, so it cannot be recovered from the artifacts.
2. **The arm is 09:21, not `replay.py`'s 09:20 window start.** Production's Executor does
   not exist at 09:20: the thesis arrives ~40 s later and the plan is derived at the next
   bar close. The cost of the wrong choice was measured on a preliminary 09:20-arm sweep —
   0 of 12 production deaths but 5 of 23 oracle deaths carried a `dol_reached` timestamp
   inside that first minute, i.e. the plan was killed over a minute production never judged
   it on. **That sweep is superseded and the figure cannot be recovered from the shipped
   artifacts**, which contain only the 09:21 run; it is recorded as the reason for the
   change, not as a result.
3. **The oracle DOL is restricted to sessions where the draw is still ahead of price at the
   arm** (`dol_behind_at_arm`, 5 sessions). The label-corpus draw is *the last pool the move
   reached*, so on part of the corpus it is already spent by the fill and is an instant
   `dol_reached` death rather than a target. The segment extreme was considered and rejected:
   it is touched by definition, so capture would be 1.0 by construction and the trail, the
   ratchet and the flip would all be untestable.
4. **The DOL menu is built at 1m resolution.** `facts_source.py`'s reason: at 1s the 17-day
   lookback is empty at the start of the study range (4 levels against 26 on 2026-05-01).
   This is the MENU's resolution and is distinct from §4 (c)'s 1m-logic / 1s-touch basis for
   the policy, which is unchanged.
5. **Plan 31's baselines were re-run and recorded as a NEW section (its §8), not as an edit
   to its §4**, which remains authoritative for 1m work per its own §7.
6. **Clause 7 is admitted on the flip bar itself.** One bar can both close beyond the target
   and close opposite by ≥3 pts; "post-flip" does not settle it. Both actions are effective
   at the same instant under clause 11 and clause 8 takes the tighter, so admitting it can
   only tighten. Recorded as an interpretation and pinned by a test; Sep-2 does not
   discriminate it.
