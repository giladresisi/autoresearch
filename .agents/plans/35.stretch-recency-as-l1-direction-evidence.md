# 35. Pre-09:20 stretch recency as an L1 direction input

> **SUPERSEDED by plan 37** (`37.deterministic-stretch-direction-override.md`), 2026-09-14.
> Kept as the record of two findings that cost real effort:
>
> 1. **A context-only facts block cannot change the direction.** `ARI_THESIS_BIAS`
>    (`validate_contracts.py:221`) requires the declared bias to match the SIGN of the
>    code-computed net score over the evidence ledger, and a context block contributes
>    0.000 to it. So it can only ever push a call toward NEUTRAL, never to the opposite
>    side. D2 was implemented, rendered correctly, and measurably moved the model — 09-01's
>    ledger went from 12 items/+13.250 to 8/+6.500, dropping all four P5 rows — and the
>    call still came out DOWN, because crossing zero needs positive points on the other
>    side and only a scored criterion can create those. **D2 has been REVERTED**; leaving it
>    would have re-keyed every recorded thesis for no reachable benefit.
> 2. **D3's premise was false.** `london(cur)_low`'s `week`/`week`/`session` tags across
>    09-01/09-02/09-04 are all CORRECT — thesis.md §2.1's running-extreme promotion. The
>    hardening that shipped from it is verified inert (54/54 identical scores across 22
>    recorded boundaries) because both production call sites already pass
>    `level_htf_close_status`, which arms the 2026-08-02 auto-derivation.
>
> **D1 (`session_stretch`) SURVIVES** and is plan 37's input. And the deeper reason 35
> could not work: scored with zero model input, 09-01's own facts produce net **-12.125**
> (expected DOWN) from the P3 mid reads alone. L1 did not misjudge that day — the repo's
> arithmetic says DOWN, strongly, and the model reported it faithfully.

🔴 Complex — the dominant cost is not the code, it is that every recorded thesis re-keys.

## Goal

Give L1 the **pre-09:20 stretch** as a direction input, and fix one evidence-quality defect
found alongside it. This plan touches the DIRECTION layer only. It must not add a gate,
a veto or a timing rule anywhere in the entry layer — see "Refuted" below.

## The measurement that motivates it

Over 94 sessions (2026-05-01..09-11, MNQ, full 09:20-13:00 coverage). The "stretch" is the
CME-session move into 09:20: from the session open (18:00 prior) to 09:20, take the high
and the low; whichever came LAST defines the stretch direction, and its age is how long
before 09:20 that extreme formed. "Reversed" = the session (09:30-13:00) ran opposite to
the stretch, where session direction is the tape's own oracle — whichever session extreme
comes first is the manipulation, so the move runs away from it.

| stretch extreme formed | sessions | reversed |
|---|---|---|
| **<= 30 min before 09:20** | 22 | **15 (68%)** |
| > 30 min before 09:20 | 72 | 35 (49%) |
| <= 15 min | 13 | 8 (62%) |
| <= 60 min | 34 | 21 (62%) |

Base rate across all 94 is 53%. **The signal is RECENCY, not size.** Conditioning on size
instead gives nothing, and at large sizes it inverts: among stretches >= 450 pts that were
not reversed to their mid, only 10/23 (43%) reversed, and >= 400 pts gives 14/34 (41%).
That inversion is why an earlier "big unreversed stretch implies a reversal is due"
formulation was dropped — it points the wrong way.

**Worked case: 2026-09-01.** Stretch DOWN 488.25 pts, extreme at 09:18 — two minutes
before the boundary. L1 called DOWN on twelve unanimous DOWN evidence items; the tape then
ran 277.25 pts UP from the 09:41:36 low. Under the recency rule this is the strongest
bucket and would have argued the other way.

n = 22 in the <= 30 min bucket, so the interval is roughly +/- 20 pp. This is a lead with a
plausible mechanism, not a settled edge, and the validation below must treat it as such.

## Refuted, and NOT to be rebuilt from this plan

Two adjacent ideas were measured and failed. They are recorded here so they are not
reintroduced:

- **Stretch recency as an ENTRY gate.** Crossing all 311 sweep-rejection fires against
  stretch freshness: on fresh-stretch days early fires score +30.38/fire against +32.61
  late, i.e. no early-window penalty. The "wait until 10:00" effect lives on the days where
  the stretch had ALREADY turned (+15.44 early vs +41.69 late). The conditioning does not
  separate the way the hypothesis expected.
- **A suppression veto keyed on the 07:00 1h FVG** (base rate: 3 firings in 94 sessions,
  two of which resolve instantly).

## Deliverables

### D1 — the stretch as a derived fact

`agent/derive_facts.py`: a `stretch` block computed at the boundary, carrying
`direction`, `size`, `extreme_ts`, `age_minutes` (boundary minus `extreme_ts`),
`mid`, `retrace_pct` (how far price has come back off the extreme as a fraction of
`size`), and `beyond_mid` (bool). Per asset — MES's own stretch is a separate reading and
§6 of `thesis.md` already governs what a two-asset disagreement means.

Derived from the same session frame the other S-sections use; no new parquet reads.

### D2 — rendering into the facts text

A dedicated block, in the style of S9's `MID POSITION`. It must state `age_minutes`
explicitly — that is the load-bearing number and the model must not have to infer it from
timestamps.

### D3 — the `london(cur)_low` tier defect (independent of D1/D2)

The same level is tagged `tier: week` on 2026-09-01 and 2026-09-02 and `tier: session` on
2026-09-04. Tier drives the P1/P2 points multiplier (session < day < week), so an
inconsistent tag silently moves score. `london(cur)_low` is a session-tier level.

Establish whether the tier is model-declared or code-derived. If model-declared, it should
be code-derived from the level name — the model's judgment is item SELECTION, not the
tier of a level whose tier is a fact. Fix at that seam.

**This is a defect fix and is independently valuable.** It should ship even if D1/D2 do not.

## THE COST, stated up front

`thesis_cache.thesis_key` hashes `facts_text`. **D2 re-keys every recorded thesis**, so
every date in the cache needs a fresh L1 call before any replay can run warm again. That
is already true of the existing cache after PR #102 (verified: 2026-08-24 misses), so the
re-seed is owed regardless — but this plan makes it mandatory rather than incidental.

Budget the re-seed explicitly before starting. Do NOT begin D2 without deciding how many
dates are to be re-seeded and accepting that cost.

## Design decision to settle BEFORE coding

**Context-only or scored criterion?**

- **(a) Context only** — render it, let the model reason. No schema change, no KB
  criterion, no validator arithmetic. Re-keys the cache but touches nothing else.
- **(b) A scored criterion (P6)** — full pipeline: `thesis.md`, `EVIDENCE_CRITERIA` in
  `agent/contracts/schemas.py`, the validator, the arithmetic override, the S-section menus.

**Recommendation: (a) first.** The effect size (68% vs 49%, n=22) does not justify a sixth
scored criterion, and (b) cannot be evaluated independently of (a) anyway — if the model
ignores the rendered fact, a points weight will not fix that, it will only mask it.

## Validation

The only honest test is direction accuracy against the tape's own oracle, A/B.

1. Pick N >= 40 dates spanning at least two months, including 2026-09-01.
2. Run L1 twice per date — with and without the D2 block — recording `bias` each time.
   `manual-l1-thesis/test_l1_thesis_manual.py` is the existing offline harness for exactly
   this and should be reused rather than rebuilt.
3. Score each `bias` against the oracle direction (first session extreme is the
   manipulation; direction runs away from it).
4. Report: accuracy with vs without, and accuracy restricted to the `age_minutes <= 30`
   subset, which is where the effect is claimed to live.
5. **Stop rule:** if accuracy on the <= 30 min subset does not improve, the block is
   removed rather than re-weighted. L1 already has twelve-item ledgers that miss; a
   thirteenth input that does not move accuracy is cost without benefit.

Note this is 2N real model calls. That is the price of testing a direction change and
there is no cheaper substitute — a cached A/B is impossible, because the change re-keys.

## Test cases

`agent/test_derive_facts_stretch.py` (new):

1. `stretch` direction is the side whose extreme came LAST (a session that made its high
   at 02:00 and its low at 09:18 is a DOWN stretch).
2. `age_minutes` is measured from the extreme to the BOUNDARY, not to the session open.
3. `retrace_pct` is 0 at the extreme and 100 at the opposite end.
4. `beyond_mid` is True when price at the boundary is still past the mid on the stretch's
   own side.
5. A session with a single monotone move yields `age_minutes == 0` and `retrace_pct == 0`.
6. Degraded input (empty frame) yields no `stretch` block rather than raising.
7. 2026-09-01 reproduces the hand-checked values: DOWN, 488.25, extreme 09:18,
   `age_minutes` 2, mid 29326.88, `beyond_mid` True.
8. MES's stretch on 2026-09-01 is computed independently and is 66.50 pts — the two assets
   disagree on magnitude by an order of magnitude, which the renderer must not hide.

For D3:

9. `london(cur)_low` resolves to `tier: session` regardless of the date.
10. A level's tier is stable across two boundaries on different days.
