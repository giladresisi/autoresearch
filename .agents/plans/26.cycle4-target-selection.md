# Cycle 4 — Target selection: what determines the draw

**Complexity: 🔴 Complex.**

Supersedes an earlier position-management framing (plan 25), whose phase-0 instrumentation was
**reverted and whose plan and execution report were deleted on 2026-09-02** — the code was
built against a theory this spec replaces, and leaving it in the tree would have misled later
work more than it helped. Its three durable findings survive in Appendix A, which is now their
only record.

---

## 1. The question

**Given that a move has started, what structural rules determine how far it goes and what
halts it — and what is the earliest moment we already knew enough to say so?**

Not "how well does the system pick targets." This cycle studies the market, not the system.
L1 and the Executor are out of the loop entirely.

### 1.1 Posture: lookahead is deliberate and total

Direction and entry are assumed correct. They belong to other modules and other cycles; the
point of assuming them is to **isolate target selection** so its rules can be found without
being confounded by entry timing or a wrong bias. Every session is studied knowing where the
move actually went.

This is the same methodology that produced `l2-mechanisms.md` — walk the chart knowing the
answer, find the structure that predicted it — pointed at the draw instead of the entry.

### 1.2 What "the target" means

**A named candidate, not a raw price.** The session's favorable extreme is a price where the
move ran out; studying it yields a distribution of distances, and distributions do not become
rules. The label is *which candidate the move drew to*, chosen from a universe identifiable
before the fact. The raw extreme is recorded alongside as the overshoot/undershoot residual.

### 1.3 What this cycle is not

- Not position management. Stops, ratchets and exits are downstream of knowing the target.
- Not a change to L1's DOL selection. Nothing in this cycle edits the Analyzer or its KB.
- Not an Executor change. No code is wired into the bar loop by this cycle's study phase.
- Not a P&L study. Rules are judged on whether they identify the drawn-to candidate, never on
  whether they improved P&L — with ~85 sessions and this many features, optimising on P&L
  fits noise.

### 1.4 Why the previous framing could not answer this

The earlier plan measured the system replaying under its own recorded theses, and 18 of 20
dates proved unreplayable because the content-addressed thesis cache had been invalidated by
KB edits. That blocker was a symptom, not an accident: the study depended on another module's
outputs to ask a question that is not about that module. Removing L1 from the loop removes the
dependency entirely, and n stops being bounded by how many recordings happen to survive.

## 2. Definitions fixed before any outcome is examined

This section exists to prevent the failure this project has paid for repeatedly: tuning a
definition until the results look good. **These are written down first and not revised in
light of outcomes.** A revision is allowed but must be dated, justified, and the whole study
re-run — never a quiet edit.

**SETTLED 2026-09-02.** Revising any row below requires a dated justification and a full
re-run — never a quiet edit.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Move-start** | the price extreme from which the studied move ran: the last counter-direction extreme before the move that the move never exceeded. Carries a price *and* a timestamp. Qualified by the existing `legs` machinery, so a trivial pullback cannot be picked as an origin |
| **Move-end** | the running extreme after which no new extreme appears within **30 minutes**. A move ends where it stops extending (§2.2) |
| **Outer window** | 09:30 → **13:00 ET**. Moves still running at 13:00 are censored; that count is reported as its own number, never folded into "the move ended here" |
| **Segmentation** | the first leg through its first qualifying counter-leg is the **primary** labelled unit; each subsequent continuation is a **secondary** segment with its own label (§4.3) |
| **The draw (DOL)** | the pool the move was **drawn to** — identified by its *completion coinciding with the turn*, **not** by proximity to move-end (§2.4). Per-instrument, and the two instruments' DOLs correspond |
| **Overshoot residual** | signed distance from the DOL to the instrument's own move-end. Recorded on every session; **never** part of identifying the DOL (§2.4) |
| **Reasonable distance** | measured **at the entry instant** — the decision instant, since distance is relative to where you stand when you decide (§2.5). Reported from both move-start price and an entry proxy; the gap between them is the *confirmation tax* |
| **Candidate universe** | both instruments' named pools plus derived structural prices, as of the evaluation instant (§3) |
| **Tolerance band** | `0.15 × avg_range_1h`, scale-free. Sensitivity-tested at 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.25. Scope is narrow: it arbitrates only whether a *near-miss* pool counts as completed. **Never applied cross-instrument** (§2.3), and **never** used to test proximity to move-end (§2.4) |
| **Ambiguous session** | two buckets, both reported, neither forced: *no move* (no leg meets the qualification floor) and *ambiguous* (two or more candidate starts or draws within the band of each other) |
| **Holdout** | block-interleaved by week, one week in four. ~22 sessions held back, ~65 for discovery |

### 2.2 Why move-end is a no-extension horizon

**REVISED 2026-09-02.** Two earlier attempts failed and both failures are instructive.

*0.382 of the move's extent* came from convention, not from anything this project justifies.
Rejected before it was built.

*The extreme terminating a leg before a qualifying counter-leg* was built, and it was wrong by
a wide margin. `legs.segment_legs` closes a leg on a retrace beyond `max(30 pts, 25%)`, and a
single MNQ 5m bar in this era routinely spans more than 30 points, so the ZigZag flips almost
every bar. Over 87 sessions it produced **~28 "moves" apiece** — one every 7.6 minutes — and on
2026-08-13 it declared the move over at 09:35 having captured 177 of the day's 404 points. The
detector is not broken; it answers the Executor's question, which is a different question.

The rule now: **a move ends where it stops extending.** Track the running extreme; the move
ends at that extreme once no new extreme appears within `NO_EXTENSION_HORIZON = 30 minutes`.

The case for 30 is that the value does not matter. At 20, 30, 45 and 60 minutes it returns
*identically* 10:35 on 2026-08-13 and 09:47 on 2026-08-11 — the two sessions whose structure
was established by hand against the charts. A parameter insensitive across a 3× range is not
what is doing the work. Contrast the retrace-fraction approach, where 08-13's own numbers had
to be measured (59.5 pts of internal drawdown against 163 after the top) before any threshold
could be defended at all.

**Direction falls out rather than being assumed.** Both directions are evaluated from the
window start; whichever terminal extreme forms LATER is the move, and the earlier opposite
extreme is its origin. On 08-13 the low prints at 09:30 and the high at 10:35 (up); on 08-11
the high prints at 09:30 and the low at 09:47 (down). Same rule, no special-casing.

### 2.4 The DOL is where the move was drawn to, not where it ended

**SETTLED 2026-09-02.** The single most consequential correction in this cycle.

An earlier draft defined the draw as the candidate nearest move-end, inside the tolerance band.
That is wrong, and 2026-08-13 disproves it: the move topped **194 points above** the highest
named MNQ pool, which no sane band admits — yet prev-week high *was* the correct DOL. It was
swept, the move continued past it, and the turn followed.

So:

- **Overshoot past the DOL is normal.** 08-13: MNQ +193.75 beyond its prev-week high, MES
  +18.25 beyond its own. Both instruments completed their objective; one ran further.
- **Falling short of the DOL is also normal**, and is handled by exit machinery — cautious
  targets, stop moves, cross-graph halt clues — **not** by re-picking the DOL, and not in this
  cycle.
- **The DOL is identified by completion coinciding with the turn**, not by proximity to the
  extreme. Move-end is evidence; it is not the label.
- **Role-swap invariance.** On 2026-08-11 MNQ swept its day low (30 pts through) while MES fell
  5 pts short — a bullish divergence — and both reversed. The DOL was the day low. Had the roles
  been reversed, MNQ's DOL would *still* be its own day low; the divergence would have said exit
  early, not that the target was different. This invariance is what makes it a selection rule
  rather than a description of what happened.

**Consequence for the null model.** "Some graph swept some pool near the turn" is a permissive
criterion — two instruments, a dozen pools each, and something is almost always swept near a
turn. §4.2's null model now points here, and it is the central methodological risk of the
cycle rather than a footnote.

### 2.5 Reasonable distance is measured at the entry instant

The decision "is this DOL at a sensible distance" is made when the position is entered — the
one-off run shortly after the fill — because distance is relative to where you stand when you
decide. Not a clock time: if the move begins at 09:41, the distance that matters is from the
price at ~09:42, where entry would occur.

This does not conflict with move-start anchoring the backward walk (§5.2). There are two
anchors doing two jobs: **move-start** anchors *when did we first know enough*, and must be
structural so sessions compare; **the entry instant** anchors *is this DOL reasonably far*.

The study has no entries, so the entry instant needs a proxy — and entry necessarily comes
*after* confirmation the move began, therefore later and at a worse price than move-start.
Distance is therefore reported **from both**, and the gap between them is the **confirmation
tax**: how much of the DOL's distance is consumed before a position can be taken.

That measurement answers a question raised in passing and otherwise unanswerable: on some days
the move begins exactly at 09:30 and much of it has passed by the time the settle window ends.
The first skeleton build puts numbers on it — **54 of 87 primary moves start in the 09:30 bin
itself, and 83 of 87 within the first 15 minutes.** Whether to allow pre-09:30 entry is out of
scope; supplying the evidence for that decision is free.

#### 2.5.1 First survey of realized distance (2026-09-03): there are no buckets

Before "reasonable" can have a number, the question was put to the corpus directly: across the
87 primary moves, how far did they actually run, and does that distance fall into shared
buckets? Realized extent (move-start to move-extreme) is an **upper bound** on the DOL distance
— overshoot inflates it (§2.4) and the entry instant sits later than move-start (§2.5) — so
every correction to it shifts the distribution *down*. That makes it a conservative basis for a
ceiling and a poor basis for a floor.

The answer is that the distance is not a property of the day's volatility regime:

| measure | median | p75 | p90 | CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw points | 253.8 | 396.3 | 487.0 | 0.637 |
| ÷ ATR5 (mean RTH range, prior 5 sessions) | 0.62 | 0.92 | 1.15 | 0.577 |
| ÷ prior day's RTH range | 0.64 | 0.94 | 1.40 | 0.819 |
| ÷ overnight range (prior 16:00 → 09:30) | 0.72 | 1.03 | 1.34 | 0.621 |

`corr(extent, ATR5) = 0.20` — volatility explains about 4% of the variance, and normalizing by
it barely tightens the spread. Prior day's range (0.23), overnight range (0.29) and gap size
(0.26) are no better. The only strong correlate is **duration (0.54)**, which is not a
predictor: it is the same lookahead move-end itself is made of.

Four candidate "common scenarios", each split at its own median, each computable at 09:30:
prior day trended (directional efficiency), prior day had a big range, wide overnight range,
gap over 30 points, primary running with vs. against the prior day's direction. **None
separates.** Every split lands between 0.60 and 0.70 median — inside the noise. Sorted, the 84
values run 0.16 → 3.12 as one smooth continuum with no visible mode structure.

**What this means for the cycle.** The negative is the finding, and it points back at the
cycle's own hypothesis: if distance is not set by the regime, it is set by *where the pools
are*. The distance to the DOL is not drawn from a distribution — it is the distance to one
specific named level, and that is whatever it is on that day. "Reasonable distance" therefore
cannot be characterized in the abstract; it is only measurable **relative to the candidate set**
(§3), after phase-2 labelling says which candidate was the draw. The per-scenario rules of
thumb remain the right shape; they must be conditioned on structure, not on volatility.

**What is already usable: a ceiling, not a bucket.** Excluding the four censored sessions
(§4.4), the 80 completed primaries have p90 = 1.07 × ATR5 (≈476 pts) and p95 = 1.16. A veto of
the form *"a candidate further than ~1.1 × ATR5 from the entry instant is out of reach"* costs
about 10% of real moves, is forward-computable at 09:30, and — because extent overstates DOL
distance — errs toward admitting rather than rejecting. That is the mirror of `DOL_FLOOR_PTS`
and the first concrete output this study owes the Planner. It is a CANDIDATE until phase 2
re-measures it against labelled DOLs rather than against extremes.

The tail is not the long-running sessions: 2026-06-25 ran 902 points in 25 minutes and closed
cleanly, while the censored four sit at 3.12, 1.61, 1.18 and 0.87 — spread across the range,
not stacked at the top.

### 2.6 Phase-2 definitions, and two amendments to the table above (2026-09-03)

Added when phase 2 was built (plan 28). The rows above stand; the two amendments at the end
are dated revisions under this section's own rule, each with the case that forced it.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Evaluation instant** | **move-start**. The universe is built at `bundle_for_boundary(..., move_start_ts)`, which slices strictly `<` the boundary — so a pool swept BY the move still reads unswept when the move begins. Structural (§2.1), proxy-free, and the same anchor §5's backward walk uses. |
| **Entry-proxy curve** | the instrument's 1m close at move-start + 2, + 5 and + 10 minutes. Reporting **only**: never eligibility, never labelling. A single proxy would be an unstated strategy choice — at + 10 on 2026-08-13 the confirmation tax is already 175.75 pts, 43% of the move. |
| **Eligible candidate** | ahead of the move's origin in the move's direction, **in that instrument's own price space**, and not already swept at the evaluation instant (§3.3). 74% of candidates are ineligible at move-start, mostly by depletion. |
| **Reached** | the instrument wick-crossed the candidate price inside the move window, engine convention (`low <= level` / `high >= level`), via `derive_facts.first_cross`. |
| **The draw, operationally** | (1) among eligible candidates **reached** in the window, the one reached **last**; (2) if none was reached, the **nearest** whose `closest_approach` is inside the band; (3) otherwise **unexplained**. "Last reached" carries no free parameter: the move ran through everything nearer and stopped at the last thing it completed. A coincidence-in-minutes would be a second 0.382. |
| **Why near-misses rank by distance, never by time** | an unreached candidate's closest approach IS the move extreme by construction, so a time-ranked rule would hand every session to a pool the move never got to. |
| **Ambiguous** | two or more distinct candidate prices crossed in the same 1m bar as the winner. Reported; the tie-break is "further along the move", which is deterministic and the only reading consistent with the move having passed through both. |
| **Counterpart status** | for the labelled candidate on instrument A, what instrument B did at the SAME named level: `reached`, `near_miss_in_band`, `near_miss_out_of_band`, `absent`. This is §3.1's phenomenon as an observation. |
| **Halt driver** | the instrument whose draw completed later of the two. Recorded, never used to relabel the other. |
| **Holdout** | ISO week number mod 4 == 0. Fixed 2026-09-03 before any rule search; 21 sessions held back over 5 interleaved weeks, 63 for discovery. `\.agents/label-corpus/holdout.json` is the record and `agent/study/holdout.py` the rule, with a test asserting they agree. |

**AMENDMENT 1 (2026-09-03): one label per INSTRUMENT, not one per session.** The table above
says the two instruments' DOLs correspond; it did not say where the label lives, and the first
implementation took a single cross-instrument argmax. 2026-08-13 refutes that: MNQ reaches its
own `prev1_week_high` at 09:46 and MES reaches its own at 09:58, so "latest wins" labels MNQ's
session with an MES price and destroys the correspondence that is the finding. Role-swap
invariance (§2.4) is now enforced structurally — each instrument is labelled in its own price
space, and the cross-instrument reading survives as `counterpart_status` and `halt_driver`.

**AMENDMENT 2 (2026-09-03): the move window ends at the END of the extreme's bar.** The
skeleton is 5m, so a segment's `extreme_ts` is the extreme BAR's open. Slicing
`[start_ts, extreme_ts]` truncates the last bar to one minute. On 2026-08-11 that bar opens
09:45 and the day-low sweep prints at 09:46: the truncated window missed it and labelled a
level 81 points short of the one the session is known for. The window is
`[start_ts, extreme_ts + grain - 1min]`.

Neither amendment was made to improve a score. Both were forced by sessions established by
hand before the rule existed, which is the only kind of evidence §2 accepts for a revision.

### 2.3 The band is never applied cross-instrument

If one asset swept a level and the other did not, that is a divergence **regardless of how far
the non-sweeping asset was** — which is already how the system treats it (`swept_at` is a
timestamp or `None`, with no threshold anywhere near it). Distance is irrelevant to that
judgement and the band must never be introduced into it.

The band's only job is within one instrument, and only for the test-and-fail case: the move
halted *short* of level L, and we want to claim L was nonetheless the draw because it pulled
price toward it and price failed to complete. `closest_approach` measures exactly that. Without
a stated threshold the claim has no discipline, which is where §4.2's null model bites hardest.

### 2.1 Move-start as the anchor, not a wall clock

Sessions are anchored at move-start rather than at a clock time. "What did we know at 09:35"
compares two days whose moves began at different times; "what did we know when the move
began" compares like with like. It is also the instant the system would actually want to
decide, since it does not want to decide at a clock time — it wants to decide when it
recognises a move is underway.

Move-start carries lookahead: you only know a move started because you know where it went.
That is accepted here and it is exactly why §2's write-it-down-first discipline applies to it
most strongly.

Defining move-start rigorously may itself yield a rule worth having — a structural
recognition of "a move is underway" is adjacent to what the entry mechanisms already attempt.
That is a welcome side effect, not a goal.

## 3. The candidate universe

### 3.1 Both instruments

MNQ frequently halts at no MNQ level at all because **MES reached one of its own**. This is
the SMT premise pointed at the target rather than the entry: the two graphs do not reach the
same levels at the same time, and that asymmetry is already treated as signal for direction.

So the universe is the union of both instruments' candidates, and the label is "which level,
on either graph, did the pair draw to."

**No price translation is needed for labelling.** Each candidate is evaluated in its own
instrument's price space at the same timestamp; MNQ's stopping price is recorded as an
observation, not a prediction. Translation between MNQ and MES price scales only becomes
necessary when an executor needs a concrete MNQ exit price from an MES-derived target — a
later phase's problem, deliberately kept out of this one.

Open item to verify, not assume: whether the facts layer currently produces a first-class MES
level universe or only MNQ's. If only MNQ's, building the MES side is part of the work.

### 3.2 Named pools *and* derived structural prices

The universe is not only liquidity pools. "The mid of the stretch the market made against the
thesis before this move" is a legitimate target and it is not in any pool list. Candidates
therefore include:

- named liquidity pools (session / day / week tiers, prior extremes, equal highs and lows)
- **derived structural prices** — mids of identified ranges, retracement levels of identified
  legs, FVG edges, the TDO

**Priority lowered 2026-09-02.** An earlier reading of 08-13 — the move topping 194 pts above
every named pool — appeared to force projected/synthetic targets into the universe as a
first-class necessity. §2.4 dissolves that: 08-13's DOL *is* prev-week high, and the 194 points
are overshoot. Named pools explain it. Derived prices stay in the universe because the stretch
mid is a real candidate class, but they are no longer on the critical path, and no
price-discovery projection machinery is required before labelling can begin.

### 3.3 Eligibility is not monotone

Knowledge accumulates; candidate validity does not. GIL-25's per-ticker depletion latch and
`compute_cautious_prices`'s `invalidated_names` mean a candidate that was live at move-start
can be dead by the time a decision fires. **Any one-off decision must re-check validity at the
instant it fires rather than trusting an earlier snapshot.**

### 3.4 HYPOTHESIS — candidates are not equally attractive

Stated 2026-09-03, to be tested in phase 2 and carried into phase 3.

If §2.5.1 is right that distance is set by *where the pools are* rather than by the day's
regime, the next question follows immediately: **some pool classes draw harder than others.**
A week-tier pool and an intraday equal-high are both candidates, and there is no reason to
expect the market to be pulled to them equally. That would explain the residual §2.5.1 could
not — why the graph travels further on some days than others is then simply how far away the
*strongly* drawing pool sat that morning, with the weakly drawing ones nearer by and ignored.

This makes attractiveness an ordering over classes, not a tie-break of last resort, and it has
a concrete consequence for phase 2's design: **the labelled corpus must record the class of
every candidate that was passed through as well as the one drawn to.** A pool the move sailed
through is evidence about that class's pull, and a design that records only the winner throws
that evidence away and cannot answer the question afterwards without a re-run.

The per-class hit rate must be reported against the class's own base rate in the universe — a
class that is 40% of all candidates and 40% of all draws has no pull, it merely has census.
This is §4.2's null model applied per class rather than in aggregate.

## 4. Labelling, and the trap in it

### 4.1 The trap

"The level that halted the move" is a judgement, and labelling by proximity-to-extreme
manufactures rules out of nothing. In a dense universe — doubled across two instruments —
*something* is always near the extreme.

### 4.2 The three disciplines that make labelling honest

1. **A stated tolerance band**, fixed in §2 before outcomes are examined.
2. **A null model.** How often does an arbitrary price land within the band of *some*
   candidate? A rule that beats chance by little is not a rule. This number must be computed
   and reported alongside every claimed hit-rate.
3. **Honest unexplained sessions.** Sessions with no candidate inside the band are reported as
   unexplained. The unexplained fraction is a headline result, not a footnote.

### 4.3 Segmentation, not an extent cap

A move allowed to run unbounded to 13:00 invites a specific false result: the study
"discovers" that some far level was the draw, when no fact available at entry could have named
it. A rule that scores well and is useless.

The obvious guard — cap the extent that counts as the move — is rejected for the same reason
0.382 was (§2.2): the cap *is* a prior on how far moves go, which is the quantity under study.
Set it tight and every session that genuinely draws three ranges gets mislabelled.

Instead the move is **segmented**. The primary labelled unit is the first leg through its first
qualifying counter-leg; each subsequent continuation is a secondary segment carrying its own
label. The primary label therefore stays clean of unanticipatable extensions — the intent
behind the cap — while nothing is defined away.

This also preserves a question a cap would have discarded: how often did the move continue past
the first halt, and was that continuation itself anticipatable from facts available at entry?
That is the same backward walk (§5) pointed one segment later, and it is where the "continue a
trade on facts discovered mid-move" question lives when it is wanted. **Recorded now, acted on
never**, until that is separately decided.

Intermediate halts within a segment are recorded as observations — a mid-move halt at an MES
level is exactly the phenomenon that motivated §3.1, and §7's switching logic will need them.

### 4.4 The long-monotone sessions are accepted as-is

Four sessions (2026-05-08, 05-12, 06-09, 08-04) have primaries still extending when the 13:00
window closes; 06-09's runs 1619.8 points over 175 minutes. One DOL for a move of that size is
unlikely to mean anything, and labelling will probably return a nonsense or unexplained label on
these four.

**Decided 2026-09-03: leave them in and let them label badly.** They are not a labelling problem
to be engineered around — they are a *position-management* problem, and the answer to them is a
stop that tightens as the trend extends (the same shape as the reverted tiered give-back of
§7.2, though not the same rule). That belongs after the system runs, where the P&L difference is
measurable in replay, and it cannot be settled from a skeleton. Excluding them here would hide
four of the most instructive sessions in the corpus; carrying them with a bad label costs 4 of
87 and keeps them visible.

Consequence to honor downstream: **the four are reported separately in every phase-2 tally.** A
rule that scores well only by explaining or failing on them is being read wrong either way.

## 5. The backward walk: earliest sufficiency

### 5.1 The question

For each session, not only "which rule identifies the draw" but "**what is the earliest
structural moment at which the evidence already supported it**".

### 5.2 A structural clock, not a wall clock

Move-start is the origin; the walk steps **backwards** through structural events — the
preceding swing, the preceding sweep, the session open, the London close — so the answer comes
out in the same vocabulary the rest of the system speaks: *"the clues were complete as of the
London sweep, two events before the move began."*

### 5.3 The fill is a comparison point, not an anchor

The fill instant is system-dependent — change an entry mechanism and it moves — so it must not
anchor anything. It is recorded only to answer one question: **did the fill fall before or
after the earliest sufficient moment?** That single fact decides whether deciding a target at
entry is feasible at all, which is the architectural question underneath this cycle.

### 5.4 This is a loop, not a pipeline

Sufficiency is undefined in the abstract: you cannot ask whether the evidence at some instant
was enough without a candidate rule to evaluate against. Rules and earliest-times are
co-determined. The work iterates — propose a rule, find its earliest sufficient moment, then
ask whether a *different* rule available earlier explains the same sessions as well — rather
than computing one and then the other.

### 5.5 What the walk decides for us

Whether the new facts can be looked up **one-off at a boundary** or must be **maintained
continuously** is not an input to this study. It is an output. If the clues are complete at or
before move-start on most sessions, a one-off snapshot is correct. If they keep arriving
mid-move, continuous evaluation is required. The architecture falls out of the measurement.

## 6. Context classes: rules are conditional

The rules being sought are not one scoring function over level attributes. They are different
rules that apply in different situations. Three seed classes, from the sessions that motivated
this cycle:

| Class | The situation | Where the target comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Stretch retracement** | the market was stretched hard counter-thesis before this move | the prior move's geometry — e.g. the mid of that stretch |
| **Unfinished business** | a pool was *almost* swept in a previous leg | that pool, made eligible by the near-miss rather than by proximity |
| **Sub-move of a larger structure** | this looks like a short reversal inside a continuation to farther liquidity | the enclosing structure's logic — e.g. reverse again at the TDO |

Conditioning is not optional. GIL-24 is the precedent: raw SMT scored 54% until daily trend
separated it into 87% and 35%. **Condition first, then measure.** A single rule fitted across
incompatible regimes produces mush.

The class list is expected to grow from the data. Classes are part of the labelling, and a
session's class must be assignable from facts available at the evaluation instant — a class
that can only be recognised in hindsight is not usable downstream.

## 7. The architecture hypothesis

> **OUT OF CYCLE 4 from 2026-09-04, except the one-off.** The cycle was narrowed to the
> fill-time target decision alone. The one-off shortly after the fill stays in scope; the
> switching rules below, §7.1's extension half and §7.2's give-back ladder all move to a later
> Executor cycle, because anything that keeps acting while the position is open is a sustained
> effort rather than a one-off. Nothing here is withdrawn — it is deferred, with its evidence.


Stated as a hypothesis the study tests, not a premise it inherits.

**One-off judgement at a boundary, deterministic mechanics thereafter.**

- Entries fire off structure that already exists, so a fill necessarily follows move-start. If
  the walk confirms the clues are complete at move-start, they are complete at the fill, and a
  **one-off decision shortly after the fill** is sound — subject to §3.3's validity re-check.
- Selecting among assembled candidates given prior-state context is a **judgement call** —
  the same kind L1 already makes for direction, and the right side of the sandwich for a
  model. The S8 eligible-DOL menu is the existing precedent for assemble-then-decide.
- **Switching during the move must be deterministic.** It happens inside the bar loop, where
  there is no wall clock and no model call.

### 7.2 CANDIDATE — tiered give-back as the exit rule

Recorded 2026-09-03. **An exit-policy candidate, explicitly NOT a move-end definition.**

The further a move has run, the less retracement it should take to exit — a young move is
allowed to breathe, a mature one is not. Proposed tiers, applied once the move is past the
secondary cautious target: extent < 150 pts → give back 0.382; 150–250 → 0.20; beyond 250 →
0.10.

It was implemented in the skeleton on 2026-09-02 and reverted the same day, and the reason is
worth keeping because it is the distinction this cycle turns on. **Move-end is what the market
did; the exit rule is what we do about it.** Used as a move-end definition the tiers truncate
the very thing being measured: median primary extent fell 253.2 → 185.0 across 87 sessions,
eleven primaries collapsed into a single 5m bar, fourteen segment pairs ran consecutively in
the same direction (a move ending and immediately continuing is a premature cut by
definition), and 2026-08-11 ended 42 points short of the day low it was drawn to — the exact
pool the study exists to identify.

The likely cause of the mismatch is the qualifier itself: "past the secondary cautious target"
cannot be evaluated in a skeleton that has no targets, so the tiers fired from a state they
were never meant to fire from. When targets exist, this becomes testable properly.

### 7.1 Tightening and extending are separate rules

Switching to a **nearer** target is protective; switching to a **farther** one is greedy. They
have different risk profiles and get separate rules with separate evidence — never one
"re-target" rule.

The legacy engine already encodes this and paid to learn it: the 09:29 re-anchor is
**tighten-only** on the secondary, and the initial is frozen entirely on an open position
because a sub-point shift to it broke a +$52 managed win on 06-16. Inherit the asymmetry
rather than rediscover it.

Switching is measured the same way the initial pick is — *was the switched-to candidate the
one actually reached* — never on whether it improved P&L.

## Appendix A — carried forward from the reverted phase 0

**This appendix is the only surviving record of these three findings** — the plan and execution
report that produced them were deleted on 2026-09-02 along with their code. All three cost real
time to discover and would cost it again.

1. **Recorded theses have a shelf life measured in KB edits.** The thesis cache key hashes the
   whole prompt including the concatenated KB, exactly as designed. Of ~20 dates wanted, two
   recordings survived. Any future study depending on "the recorded thesis for date D" must
   either re-seed or accept that its era is whatever the KB was on the day it ran.
2. **The 1s driver hands the current second's bar, not the in-progress minute**, contrary to
   `Executor._death`'s docstring. An offline reconstruction that floors to the minute reports a
   different excursion than the online tracker — caught only by a conformance check between
   the two representations. This study reconstructs paths offline and would have hit it too.
3. **The existing decisions artifacts cannot serve as a corpus.** Of 594 `trader_decisions.jsonl`
   files, 318 carry injected oracle theses including deliberately inverted ones; the clean
   remainder covers three dates.
